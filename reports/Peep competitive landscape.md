# Peep Wins by Closing the Whole Giving Loop

No company does everything Peep plans to do. Peep would take any nonprofit PDF and turn it into a standardized campaign one-pager, match brands to campaigns by giving budget and cause, move the money, confirm it arrived, and issue a receipt that fits the deal. Each step exists somewhere, but in five separate categories. **CSR suites** (Benevity, Bonterra/Deed, Blackbaud YourCause, Groundswell, Uncommon Giving, Millie) move corporate money but are built for employee giving and inbound grant applications. **Sponsorship software and marketplaces** (SponsorUnited, SponsorCX, Trak, SponsorMind, OpenSponsorship, SponsorMyEvent, Passionfroot) handle tiers, matching and payments, but for sports, events and creators, and they treat every deal as a marketing purchase. **Nonprofit fundraising tools** (Zeffy, Givebutter, GoFundMe Pro, Donorbox, Instrumentl, Candid) help nonprofits convert donors they already have or find foundation grants. They bring in no brand money. **Giving infrastructure** (Every.org, Pledge, Change, PayPal Giving Fund, Chariot, Stripe Connect) moves and receipts money, but nobody sees it. **Brand-asset tools** (Brandfetch, Frontify, Canva, Cvent's exhibitor portals) store logos but have no link to funding. The nearest single analogs are Hello Good's agency-run GoodMatch and a string of nonprofit sponsorship marketplaces that have died. That history shows both the opening and the main risk: brands have never reliably shown up to self-serve. Peep's real advantage is not AI page generation, which incumbents already ship. It is a transaction layer that understands taxes, sitting under a cause-first marketplace. The 2026 1% corporate deduction floor makes that layer more valuable just as it starts to bite. The founder's brand asset vault fits on top: unlocking it with a verified payment ties the money and the brand relationship together, something no competitor does.

## Five adjacent categories each cover one link of Peep's loop

The useful way to read this market is by which step of Peep's loop each player owns. Peep's loop has six steps: ingest the nonprofit's materials, standardize them into a campaign, match the campaign to a brand budget, move the funds, verify and receipt them, and support an optional relationship afterward. Every competitor below owns one or two steps. None owns more than three.

### CSR suites have the networks but serve HR, not brand budgets

The incumbents are large. Benevity has supported **$36B+ to 560,000+ nonprofits since 2008** and manages $2B+ in grants a year ([Yahoo Finance / Benevity Live! 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/technology/articles/benevity-unveils-ai-native-csr-134000816.html)). Bonterra bought Deed on **March 5, 2026**, says it powers grant programs for one-third of the Fortune 100 ([Bonterra](https://www.bonterratech.com/news-press/bonterra-acquires-deed)), and launched Bonterra Deed in July with access to 10M+ nonprofits ([Bonterra](https://www.bonterratech.com/news-press/bonterra-launches-deed)). Pricing puts them out of reach for the long tail. Vendr's median Benevity contract is about **$48,733/yr** ([Vendr](https://www.vendr.com/marketplace/benevity)). Even the challengers charge before a dollar moves: Uncommon Giving from $5,000/yr ([Uncommon Giving](https://uncommongiving.com/pricing/)), Groundswell from $8,500/yr ([Groundswell](https://groundswell.io/pricing)), Givinga from $12,500/yr ([Givinga](https://www.givinga.com/pricing)).

Their weaknesses line up with Peep's pitch. Nonprofit content on these platforms is a self-written profile with logo, mission and tags, not a standardized campaign with a goal and tiers ([Benevity for Nonprofits](https://benevity.com/nonprofits)). Their 2026 AI work goes to admin efficiency: grant summaries, rubric scoring, and Match Assurance, which cut external match decline rates from 30% to under 3% ([Benevity](https://benevity.com/press-releases/global-grantmaking-ai-management-features)). YourCause is only now promising personalized recommendations for *employees* ([Blackbaud](https://www.blackbaud.com/newsroom/article/pubs-may-2026)). Disbursement is their weak spot. YourCause's standard payout takes **20–60 days** ([Uncommon Giving comparison, competitor-authored](https://uncommongiving.com/comparison/yourcause/)). Benevity's Capterra score is **3.4/5, with 2.9 for customer service**, and one reviewer wrote: "I have no independent confirmation that the ACS actually received my donation" ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/155271/Benevity/reviews/)). Bonterra's own launch copy admits that "acquired and assembled" suites produce "three exports and a spreadsheet" ([Bonterra](https://www.bonterratech.com/news-press/bonterra-launches-deed)).

The closest thing to Peep's matching on the corporate side is **Hello Good's GoodMatch** (Feb 2026). It scores brand-nonprofit pairs on cause alignment, audience overlap, geography, budget fit and values, then makes human warm introductions ([Hello Good](https://hellogood.club/news/ai-cause-marketing)). It is agency-led, with no self-serve flow and no payment handling. It proves the matching logic, not the marketplace.

### Sponsorship software has the tiers and proof loops but not the charity layer

Money and data in sponsorship sit in professional sports. SponsorUnited raised a **$35M Series A** ([The Daily Upside](https://www.thedailyupside.com/technology/sponsor-data-startup-raises-35-million-in-venture-capital/)) and claims **$45B+ in verified deal data**. In February 2026 it launched a Proposal Evaluator that lets brands "upload proposals for a detailed analysis" ([SponsorUnited](https://www.sponsorunited.com/platform); [StreetInsider](https://www.streetinsider.com/Press+Releases/SponsorUnited+Redefines+Sponsorship+Decision+Making+with+Breakthrough+AI-Powered+Innovation/25952129.html)). That is the only "upload your PDF" feature in the category, and it scores proposals for buyers rather than turning them into polished listings. SponsorCX extended its Series A in May 2026 ([Utah Business](https://www.utahbusiness.com/press-releases/2026/05/21/sponsorcx-secures-series-a-expansion-kickstart-ai-powered-sponsorship/)). It runs an activation portal with artwork approvals and proof of performance for properties such as the USL ([RFP.wiki](https://www.rfp.wiki/vendors/sponsorcx)). Trak serves the brand side from **$7,000/yr** and tracks "community investment" alongside sponsorship ([Trak](https://www.trak.io/pricing)), which shows that a cause-sponsorship budget line exists inside companies. The most direct down-market threat is **SponsorMind**, which joined GSIC in May 2026. Its suite for small and mid-sized event organizers covers matching (SponsorHitch), proposals (SponsorDeck) and payments (SponsorPay) ([GSIC](https://www.sport-gsic.com/blog/new-members-3/sponsormind-joins-gsic-bringing-industrytrained-ai-to-transform-the-global-sponsorship-ecosystem-137)).

Marketplaces show how Peep's transaction layer can work. OpenSponsorship holds brand payments in escrow until deliverables are approved ([OpenSponsorship](https://help.opensponsorship.com/en/articles/763001-how-do-you-guarantee-that-the-athlete-other-talent-sticks-to-the-terms-in-my-campaign)). Passionfroot charges brands **5% on storefront bookings and 15% on sourced deals**, keeps creators free, and raised **$15M in July 2026** ([Passionfroot](https://help.passionfroot.me/creators/getting-started/pricing); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/22/passionfroot-raises-15m-to-expand-its-b2b-creator-marketplace-to-the-us/)). Collabstr adds a 10% brand-side fee and handles tax and invoicing ([Collabstr](https://collabstr.com/pricing)). None of these platforms verifies 501(c)(3) status, issues charitable receipts, or separates a qualified sponsorship payment from advertising.

The nonprofit-specific attempts are a warning. Sponsorist ([THAT Agency](https://thatagency.com/blog/behind-the-scenes-at-sponsorist-com-interview-with-rosston-meyer/)), Philanthropic.ly, which matched sponsorships to company giving initiatives ([3BL Media](https://www.3blmedia.com/news/introducing-philanthropicly-new-giving-platform-creating-meaningful-connections-between)), Groupable and Sponsorfied ([TechCrunch 2012](https://techcrunch.com/2012/07/25/sponsorfied/)) all appear inactive. ZipSprout survives as a brokered matcher motivated by local SEO. It has placed **$9.78M across 29,904 sponsorships since 2016**, about **$327 per deal** ([ZipSprout](https://zipsprout.com/nonprofits-events-organization-sign-up/)). Brand-to-nonprofit matching has so far meant small tickets bought for marketing reasons, not budgeted giving.

### Nonprofit tools race to zero fees and AI drafting, but bring no brands

Nonprofit campaign tools are cheap, polished and getting smarter. Zeffy is free to nonprofits, funded by donor tips, and claims **100,000 organizations** ([Zeffy](https://www.zeffy.com/pricing)). Small nonprofits already sell sponsorship tiers on it as ticket types with free-text benefits at **$250–$1,500** ([Zeffy example](https://www.zeffy.com/en-US/ticketing/sfw-4th-annual-golf-outing-sponsorships-and-donations)). Givebutter Magic turns a nonprofit's *website* into a draft campaign ([Givebutter](https://givebutter.com/magic)). GoFundMe Pro shipped 40+ updates in H1 2026, including AI copy built from a brand kit, reusable templates and DAFpay ([GoFundMe Pro](https://pro.gofundme.com/c/blog/new-on-gofundme-pro-h1-2026/)). These are the features most likely to be copied toward Peep's ingestion step. Still, no tool found converts an uploaded deck or PDF into a page. Grant tools such as Grantable and Instrumentl ingest documents but output proposals, not public pages ([Grantable](https://grantable.co/); [Instrumentl](https://www.instrumentl.com/pricing)).

What none of them supplies is brand demand. Neon One says it plainly: "there is no big corporate sponsorship directory" ([Neon One](https://neonone.com/resources/blog/corporate-sponsorships-for-nonprofits/)). Double the Donation's 24,000-company sponsorship database is a research tool, not a marketplace ([Double the Donation](https://doublethedonation.com/sponsorships-demo/)). Free funder discovery has also become commoditized: Candid made its unified search free in January 2026, then laid off about a fifth of its staff ([GrantCue](https://www.grantcue.com/blog/is-foundation-directory-being-discontinued)). Data alone is not a moat. Transaction rails, verification and receipts are where value is moving.

### Infrastructure and asset tools are parts Peep should license, not fight

Giving infrastructure is cheap and commoditized. Stripe Connect costs **$2 per active account per month plus 0.25% + $0.25 per payout** ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/fr-us/connect/pricing)). IRS Pub 78, BMF and auto-revocation files are free monthly downloads ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/tax-exempt-organization-search-bulk-data-downloads)). Candid's audit-grade Charity Check, with OFAC screening, starts at **$2,750** ([Candid](https://candid.org/explore-apis/)). Receipting intermediaries such as Every.org, Pledge (3% + $5 per beneficiary + processing, per [RallyUp](https://rallyup.com/learn/understand-pledge/)), Change and PayPal Giving Fund (15–45 days to enrolled charities, per [PayPal](https://paypal.com/bh/cshelp/article/when-and-how-does-paypal-giving-fund-grant-donations-to-charities-help969)) receive the gift, issue the receipt and re-grant it. All of them are invisible to the funder.

Brand-asset tools are just as fragmented. Brandfetch lets brands claim a **free public profile** that syncs to 100+ partner sites, but it has no per-recipient permissions ([Brandfetch](https://brandfetch.com/why-brandfetch)). Frontify charges per monthly active user, including external partners ([KI-Syndikat](https://www.ki-syndikat.de/tools/frontify/)), so the cost grows with every nonprofit that logs in. Canva only allows org-wide Brand Kit sharing on Enterprise ([Canva Help](https://www.canva.com/en_gb/help/brand-kit/)). Cvent makes sponsors re-upload and crop logos for every event ([Tango/Cvent](https://app.tango.us/app/workflow/How-to-Add-Logo--Update-Company-Information-in-the-Cvent-Exhibitor-Portal-3ff98cf747a54773a8fdbee6c4099cd5)). Greenfly moves content the other way, from rights-holder to sponsor ([Greenfly](https://www.greenfly.com/uncategorized/sponsors-activating-partnerships-using-greenfly/)).

## The white space is budget-first, cause-first giving with tax-correct paperwork

Five gaps stand out in the evidence, and together they make up Peep's differentiator.

**First, the campaign is not a standard unit anywhere.** Incumbents index organizations by EIN. Nobody normalizes a nonprofit's ask into a comparable page with goal, progress, budget and tiers. Practitioners say big attached decks get ignored ([Nunify](https://www.nunify.com/blogs/how-to-find-sponsors-for-an-event)). Almost half of nonprofits reportedly have **no staff dedicated to corporate sponsorship**, and **49% say finding companies to partner with is their top corporate priority** ([Double the Donation](https://doublethedonation.com/corporate-sponsorship-statistics/), search excerpt only). A free "upload any PDF" step saves real labor, and the standard format is what lets brands compare causes side by side.

**Second, nobody matches a company's own budget to campaigns by cause.** Sponsorship marketplaces match on audience and reach. CSR tools recommend causes to employees or score inbound applications. Brands are swamped by unstructured asks: Canon Canada says it gets "tens of thousands" of requests a year ([Canon Canada](https://canon.ca/en/About-Canon/Donations-and-Sponsorships/Brand-Sponsorship-Inquiry)). Some pay screening services such as International Sponsor Network to filter them ([Newswire](https://www.newswire.com/news/international-sponsor-network-gears-up-for-expansion-158529)). Small shops filter on personal relevance and decline anything with less than about three weeks' notice ([MIT case study](https://wikis.mit.edu/confluence/x/XEUkBQ)). A brand that sets a budget, picks causes and geography, and gets a vetted shortlist turns that flood into a feed.

**Third, the SMB and mid-market brand has no product.** Corporate giving reached **$43.67B in 2025, about 7% of total US giving** ([Candid](https://candid.org/blogs/philanthropy-trends-giving-usa/)). It was nearly flat after inflation ([Inpact](https://inpact.com/blog/the-most-interesting-number-in-giving-usa-2026-isnt-the-record/)), and **52% of large companies still cut giving in 2025** while the top quartile drove the growth ([3BL / CECP](https://www.3blmedia.com/news/giving-numbersr-2026-edition-data-reveals-stark-divide-corporate-giving)). Below the enterprise tier, a 20–500 person company that wants to "give $X to causes we care about" has two choices: an employee-engagement SaaS costing $5K or more, or writing checks with no vetting, matching or reporting. Only **28% of small and mid-size companies offer matching gifts, versus 65% of the Fortune 500** ([Access Philanthropy](https://accessphilanthropy.com/what-motivates-donors-to-give/)).

**Fourth, the funder cannot see that the money arrived.** Benevity's reviewers complain about this, YourCause takes 20–60 days, and **26% of 2024 checkout campaigns did not clearly identify the benefiting charity** ([NonProfit PRO](https://www.nonprofitpro.com/article/2025-charity-checkout-champions-report-reveals-most-effective-campaigns/)). A verified-arrival page with a receipt fixes a trust problem that has been documented again and again.

**Fifth, no platform handles the tax line between a gift and a sponsorship, and in 2026 that line matters more.** Since tax years beginning after December 31, 2025, corporations can deduct charitable gifts only above **1% of taxable income**, still capped at 10% ([Pierce Atwood](https://www.pierceatwood.com/alerts/congress-imposes-1-floor-corporate-charitable-deductions)). Amounts disallowed by the floor carry forward only when giving also exceeds the 10% cap ([Greenberg Traurig](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2025/10/new-limitations-on-charitable-deductions-take-effect-in-2026)). The median large company gives about **0.39% of pre-tax profit**, below the floor ([Candid](https://candid.org/blogs/corporate-social-investments-cecp-data/)). A payment can be deducted under §170 (charitable gift) or §162 (business expense), not both. Business expenses face no floor, but they need a documented business purpose and an expected return ([Bloomberg Tax](https://news.bloombergtax.com/daily-tax-report/new-corporate-charitable-donation-rule-is-a-double-edged-sword)). On the nonprofit side, IRC §513(i) keeps a payment out of taxable advertising income only if the sponsor gets nothing more than acknowledgment: name, logo, a value-neutral tagline. Comparative claims, prices, endorsements and calls to action count as advertising ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/node/13726)). Benefits worth more than **2% of the payment** are counted in full, not just the excess ([federal-regs.com](https://federal-regs.com/title/26/part-1/1.513-4/)). Donors need written acknowledgment for gifts of $250 or more, and quid pro quo payments over $75 need a disclosure ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/Charities-&-Non-Profits/Charitable-Organizations/Charitable-Contributions-Written-Acknowledgments); [IRS Pub 1771](https://www.irs.gov/node/551)). No competitor found builds any of this into its product.

Taken together, Peep's differentiator is **one closed loop**: a campaign format the nonprofit approves, budget-and-cause matching, money movement with visible proof, and paperwork that matches what the brand actually received. Each competitor would have to build three of those layers to copy it. The founder's asset vault then becomes the part brands interact with that holds the loop together.

There is also an angle Peep should take a position on. The 1% floor works against pure corporate gifts for most companies. A typical company under the floor gets **no deduction for a gift but a full deduction for a sponsorship that qualifies and is documented as a business expense** (an inference that needs tax counsel to confirm case by case). A marketplace that can paper both, letting brands choose between a "gift" with acknowledgment only and a "sponsorship" invoiced by the nonprofit with broader recognition, serves brands whatever their tax position. The incumbents' receipting is built for employees and DAFs and cannot do this.

## Starting from the asset vault, twenty-four features that make Peep hard to copy

The founder's idea is a brand profile plus an asset vault (logos, brand guide, mission and boilerplate, approved photos, usage rules) that any funded nonprofit can pull from, and through which brands can be pitched. The evidence supports it. Logo collection today is manual and error-prone. Events demand vector files with fonts outlined and drop logos that don't comply ([Novogradac](https://www.novoco.com/events/sponsorship-logo-requirements)). A sign shop reports receiving "a scanned business card" as a logo and re-tracing it with about 60% success ([Signs101](https://signs101.com/threads/sponsorship-banners.157429), anecdotal). Sponsors re-upload for every event ([Tango/Cvent](https://app.tango.us/app/workflow/How-to-Add-Logo--Update-Company-Information-in-the-Cvent-Exhibitor-Portal-3ff98cf747a54773a8fdbee6c4099cd5)). Brands want the recognition handled correctly.

Storage alone is easy to copy. Brandfetch or any DAM could host a kit. What makes the vault Peep's is what it connects to: payments, verification, tax rules and the matching feed. Three of these deserve emphasis because they protect the vault from its own failure modes. **Pitch quotas (#5)** matter because a vault that "brands can be pitched through" could recreate the inbound flood brands are escaping. ## Brand-side demand is the risk that killed every predecessor

**The biggest risk is that brands don't show up.** Every earlier nonprofit sponsorship marketplace (Sponsorist, Philanthropic.ly, Groupable, Sponsorfied) appears inactive, and the one survivor, ZipSprout, runs on brokered SEO deals averaging about $327. Venture money in 2025–2026 went to sports intelligence (SponsorUnited, SponsorCX) and B2B creator marketplaces (Passionfroot), not to nonprofit sponsorship marketplaces. That points to white space, and also to how investors judge small-ticket nonprofit demand. Nonprofit supply will be easy to get: free PDF-to-page conversion appeals to the roughly half of nonprofits without sponsorship staff. A marketplace full of polished one-pagers and no budgets would repeat Sponsorist. Peep should acquire brands budget-first. Three reasons to join stand on their own: the 1% floor calculator, the "send them to Peep" request link, and the vault as a free logo-request killer. Peep can also seed early demand by brokering matches by hand, as Sponsorfied and ZipSprout did.

**The 1% floor cuts both ways.** Candid expects the floor's impact to "be felt in the reduction of contributions from small and midsize businesses" ([Candid](https://candid.org/blogs/corporate-social-investments-cecp-data/)). That is the segment Peep targets. If pure gifts lose their tax value, some SMBs will stop giving. This is why the gift-or-sponsorship switch is essential: it keeps Peep useful to a brand whichever deduction path it takes.

**Incumbents can copy individual features, but not the whole loop quickly.** Givebutter already turns websites into campaigns, and GoFundMe Pro has AI copy plus brand kits. Either could add PDF ingestion within months. Benevity and Bonterra hold the largest vetted networks and payment rails and could add a "company campaigns" module. SponsorMind is moving down-market with matching, decks and payments in one suite. The defense is that each would have to build three layers outside its core: tax-aware receipting, funding-gated assets, and budget-first brand matching. Each is also held back by its own business. CSR suites sell to HR on employee participation, and nonprofit tools depend on donor tips. Peep should assume ingestion will be copied within 12 months and put its moat in the transaction and relationship layers.

**Legal structure is a risk to the product's design, not just a compliance cost.** The simplest receipting model routes funds through an intermediary 501(c)(3) or DAF, as Every.org, Pledge and Change do. That model conflicts with sponsorship benefits. IRC §4967 imposes a **125% excise tax** on a donor-advisor who gets a more-than-incidental benefit from a DAF distribution ([GovInfo](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2021-title26-subtitleD-chap42-subchapG-sec4967.htm)). IRS Notice 2017-73 treated event attendance as a prohibited benefit ([Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/newsroom/alerts/2017/12/IRS-Notice-Provides-Guidance-on-use-of-Donor-Advised-Funds-for-Pledges-and-Charity-Events)). Peep likely needs a hybrid: pure gifts through an intermediary, and sponsorships paid directly to the nonprofit through Stripe Connect, with the nonprofit issuing the acknowledgment or invoice. On top of that, California AB 488 requires charitable fundraising platforms to register, hold funds in a separate account and disclose maximum time to disbursement ([CA OAG](https://oag.ca.gov/node/584000)). About 24 states regulate commercial co-venturers when a brand publicly ties purchases to charity ([Labyrinth](https://labyrinthinc.com/cause-marketing-commercial-co-venture-guide-alabama/), single vendor source). The gift-bunching feature would make Peep hold funds across tax years, which may require a DAF sponsor structure.

**Monetization has to avoid the tip trap.** Nonprofits now expect free: Zeffy, Givebutter with tips on, and Every.org charge them nothing, which sets a 0% benchmark. Pre-checked tips backfire: Zeffy's 15–17% defaults draw "sneaky" complaints ([CheckThat](https://checkthat.ai/brands/zeffy/reviews)), and Givebutter faces a proposed class action over undisclosed fees filed August 4, 2026 ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/software-platform-for-nonprofits-hit-with-deceptive-fees-suit)). The Passionfroot and Collabstr pattern fits Peep: a visible fee charged to the brand on top of the gift, with funding by ACH to avoid 2–3% card costs. Willingness to pay was not researched, so the fee level is still a guess.

**Trust can break overnight.** GoFundMe's auto-generated pages showed that standardizing nonprofit pages without consent destroys goodwill fast ([Chronicle of Philanthropy](https://www.philanthropy.com/news/backlash-grows-as-charities-say-gofundmes-donation-pages-create-chaos/)). Peep's one-pager has to stay something the nonprofit starts and approves.

## A roadmap that proves brand demand before building tax machinery

The order of work should follow the main risk. Ship what pulls brands in and what makes the first transactions clean. Defer the features that need fund-holding structures or a large network.

| Phase | Features | Why now |
|---|---|---|
| **Now (0–3 months), in time for year-end 2026 giving** | PDF→one-pager with nonprofit approval (#17); brand profile + vault v1 with Brandfetch pre-fill (#1); "send them to Peep" request link (#6); 1% floor calculator, display only (#9A); explainable budget/cause/geo matching (#11); verified-arrival page on Stripe Connect (#10); IRS bulk-file verification | Brings in both sides at near-zero cost; the floor calculator is a timely, standalone brand hook as calendar-year companies close their first tax year under the floor; nothing here requires Peep to hold funds |

It needs counsel sign-off on receipt language and the hybrid payment flow first |
| **Later (9–18 months)** | Gift bunching and scheduled disbursement via a DAF partner (#9B); fiscal-year calendar (#14); story kit (#13); local feed and pooled circles (#18); SMB matching/employee voting (#19); Peep Pledge badge (#20); impact digest (#21); switchable multi-year (#22); disaster pool (#24); DAM importer | Needs fund-holding structure, AB 488/CCV review, or a network large enough to make pooling, voting and local feeds worthwhile |

The asset vault belongs in the first release even though its gated version comes later. A free, well-structured logo and boilerplate page is the cheapest reason for a brand to create a profile. Every brand profile is a demand-side account that can later receive matches. The vault is how Peep acquires brands, as well as a feature for them.

## Where the evidence is thin

Much of this report rests on search-result snippets, not full-page reads. WebFetch failed with DNS errors for most of the sponsorship-marketplace research (pricing for SponsorPitch, SponsorMyEvent, OpenSponsorship and Passionfroot came from snippets and aggregators). The same is true of the brand-asset and giving-infrastructure research, including IRS pages, Every.org, Pledge and Cornell's legal texts. Primary market sources (Candid, Chronicle of Philanthropy, the Lilly School, Double the Donation) were also read through excerpts. The **Double the Donation nonprofit statistics** (47% no strategy, ~50% no staff, 49% top priority, 41% timing) come from a vendor survey of unnamed method, seen only in a search excerpt. **SMB giving data** is weak: the SCORE "66% give locally" figure dates from 2019 and cites an unnamed survey. The only **cause-sponsorship market size** found is IEG's $2.14B projection for 2018. **Corporate giving totals conflict**: $43.67B per Candid versus $46.4B per BWF's summary.

Several claims central to Peep's strategy are unverified. No quantitative data exists on how many hours nonprofits spend chasing sponsor logos; that evidence is anecdotal. No one has measured brand willingness to pay for a vault or a platform fee. No one has measured whether SMBs will self-serve. The notes did not confirm whether Benevity, YourCause or Bonterra offer grantees a brand-kit library, so Peep should check before claiming to be "first." On the legal side, four points need tax counsel: whether logo acknowledgment counts as "goods or services" on a §170 receipt, whether Notice 2017-73's DAF event-benefit approach was finalized, whether AB 488 covers B2B matching, and how the floor's carryforward works. Competitor pricing and complaints for YourCause and Bonterra come partly from Uncommon Giving, a direct competitor. First-hand nonprofit sentiment from Reddit could not be collected.

## Conclusion

Peep looks at first like a sponsorship marketplace, a category with a record of failure. The evidence suggests a better framing: Peep is a **tax-aware giving and recognition system** for companies too small for Benevity and too cause-driven for SponsorUnited. The 1% floor drives this. It turns "gift or sponsorship?" from an accounting footnote into a decision that changes how much a brand can deduct, and no incumbent is set up to help brands make it. A platform that writes the correct paperwork for either choice, and attaches recognition to the money through a funding-gated vault, offers something brands cannot get anywhere else.

The practical implication is that Peep's moat sits in the least glamorous layers: receipts, verification snapshots, placement proofs and usage rules. Givebutter and GoFundMe will likely copy the PDF-to-page step that makes the demo shine. The vault is valuable because it turns every funded relationship into a permissioned, auditable record that grows with each transaction, and copying that requires building payments, compliance and brand trust together. The test that decides the company is whether brands set budgets when nobody is brokering the deal. The first roadmap phase should be judged on that number, not on nonprofit sign-ups.

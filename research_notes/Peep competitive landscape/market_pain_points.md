# Market Size, Trends and Unmet Needs in Corporate Giving and Nonprofit Sponsorship (for Peep)

Research date: 2026-10-23. US focus. Method note: several primary domains (candid.org, philanthropy.com, philanthropy.indianapolis.iu.edu, doublethedonation.com, bnnbloomberg.ca) could not be fetched from this environment because of DNS failures. Many figures below therefore come from search-result excerpts of those pages or from secondary coverage, not from a full read of the primary report. ## 1. Market size: US corporate giving, cause sponsorship, SMB giving, and 2024-2026 trends (tax, DEI, greenhushing)

### Takeaway
US corporate giving was about $43.7B in 2025, roughly 7% of the $617B total. It was flat after inflation, and the growth is concentrated in a small number of large companies (top-quartile CECP firms). About half of large companies cut giving. The median company gives only about 0.39% of pre-tax profit, which is below the new 1% deduction floor that started for tax years beginning after 2025. That floor, together with DEI/ESG caution ("quiet" giving), is reshaping who gives and how. Small and mid-size businesses (SMBs) are the group most exposed to the floor. That is both a risk and an opening for a product that helps them bunch gifts and keep them simple.

**SMB giving behavior**

**Tax law: OBBBA (signed July 2025)**
- Corporate charitable contributions are deductible only to the extent they exceed 1% of taxable income, up to the existing 10% cap. This applies to tax years beginning after Dec. 31, 2025. — [Pierce Atwood alert](https://www.pierceatwood.com/alerts/congress-imposes-1-floor-corporate-charitable-deductions); [Greenberg Traurig, Oct 2025](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2025/10/new-limitations-on-charitable-deductions-take-effect-in-2026); [Bipartisan Policy Center explainer](https://bipartisanpolicy.org/explainer/the-one-big-beautiful-bill-acts-changes-to-charitable-deductions/)
- One summary says amounts disallowed by the floor do not carry forward. This was not verified against the statute; some advisers describe a limited carryforward when giving also exceeds the 10% cap. — [Roundpaper: 2026 rules explained](https://www.roundpaper.com/nonprofits/resources/charitable-deduction-rules-2026); see also [Stanford giving explainer](https://giving.stanford.edu/stories/the-impact-of-the-one-big-beautiful-bill-act-on-charitable-giving)
- Individuals, same law: starting 2026, a new non-itemizer deduction ($1,000 single / $2,000 joint) and a 0.5%-of-AGI floor for itemizers. This matters for owners of pass-through SMBs, where giving flows through to owners' personal returns. — [Bipartisan Policy Center](https://bipartisanpolicy.org/explainer/the-one-big-beautiful-bill-acts-changes-to-charitable-deductions/); [Reed tax guide](https://reedcorp.tax/helpful-guides/2026-charitable-deduction-changes/)

**DEI pullback and greenhushing (2025-2026)**
- Among S&P 500 companies, use of the acronym "DEI" in filings dropped 68% versus 2024. Companies are "reframing, not abandoning." — [ESG Dive on Conference Board, 2025](https://www.esgdive.com/news/major-companies-reframing-not-abandoning-dei-2025-report-the-conference-board-trump/757419/)
- Conference Board: corporate philanthropy leaders are already adjusting. Risk-averse firms may pull back even on giving that remains legal. — [CNBC, Feb 2025](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/02/16/in-national-dei-dismantle-corporate-charities-are-in-for-scrutiny.html)
- One in eight companies planned to weaken DEI commitments in 2025. — [ESG Dive, 2025](https://www.esgdive.com/news/companies-to-weaken-dei-commitments-in-2025/738608/)
- Greenhushing: companies "downplay—but not divest" climate and equity commitments, avoid the term "ESG," or stop reporting initiatives. — [Future 500](https://www.future500.org/blog/https/wwwfuture500org/blog/greenhushing)
- Minnesota Council of Nonprofits survey (more than 300 groups, 2025): leaders reported layoffs as DEI attacks "chilled corporate giving." Nearly half saw lower government funding and 35% saw lower individual donations. — [Sahan Journal, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/democracy-politics/nonprofits-funding-cuts-dei-rollback-minnesota/)

### Inferences
- The 1% floor creates a strong incentive to bunch gifts. A company giving below 1% of taxable income each year gets no deduction. Giving, say, 2-3% every other year (or front-loading into one tax year) restores deductibility. The median CECP company (0.39%) is under the floor, and most SMBs probably are too. That supports Peep's tax-planning premise: a "budget set by tax planning" plus timing advice (what to give in which tax year, and how far above the floor) is a real, new need from 2026. Multi-year matching (fund once, disburse to several nonprofits over 12-24 months) could be a distinctive product. This is my inference; confirm with tax counsel, especially on carryforward.
- Corporate giving is concentrating in large companies with in-house CSR teams and platforms (Benevity, CECP members). The long tail of SMBs is underserved and may be shrinking. A marketplace that makes SMB giving simple and tax-efficient is filling a gap, not competing head-on with Benevity.
- High grantee churn (about two-thirds of companies changed whom they fund) and quieter, lower-profile giving point to demand for low-visibility, low-commitment, easily switched giving. That fits Peep's "communication optional" premise. It also means public recognition must be opt-in per brand.
- Calendar-year C corps were not subject to the floor on 2025 gifts, so late 2025 probably saw some acceleration. No data confirms this yet; Giving USA 2027 would show it.

### Gaps
- No public 2024-2026 IEG/WARC figure for cause sponsorship spend (IEG data now sits behind a WARC paywall).
- No rigorous recent survey of SMB giving (share giving, average amount, channels). The SCORE "250%" figure is from 2019 and unsourced.
- No reliable national figure for the share of US nonprofits that receive any corporate support, or for corporate money as a share of nonprofit revenue. Candid/Foundation Directory data may hold this, but candid.org could not be reached.
- No measured data yet on the 1% floor's effect on 2026 giving or 2025 pull-forward.

## 2. Nonprofit pain points in seeking corporate sponsorship

### Takeaway
Nonprofits are financially strained (36% ran deficits in 2024, a 10-year high in NFF data) while corporate dollars concentrate in fewer, larger companies. Roughly half lack a sponsorship strategy or dedicated staff. Finding partners and meeting corporate timing cycles are the top problems. That supports the "level the playing field" premise, but evidence on deck design specifically is anecdotal.

### Cited Findings
- NFF 2025 State of the Nonprofit Sector (2,206 nonprofits, fielded Jan to Mar 2025, covering 2024): 36% ended 2024 with an operating deficit, the highest in 10 years of the survey. 85% expected demand to increase in 2025. Many couldn't meet 2024 demand. — [NFF national release](https://nff.org/insights/survey-us-nonprofits-at-critical-point-as-funding-for-community-needs-falters/); [NFF 2025 Executive Summary PDF](https://nff.org/wp-content/uploads/NFF-2025-Survey-Executive-Summary-1.pdf); [NFF methodology](https://nff.org/state-of-the-nonprofit-sector-survey/2025-state-of-the-survey-nonprofit-sector-survey/2025-survey-methodology/)
- The Chronicle described sectors "close to reaching a breaking point" amid federal funding pullbacks. — [Chronicle of Philanthropy](https://www.philanthropy.com/news/colliding-crises-are-straining-nonprofit-finances-survey-finds/)
- Blackbaud Institute: small organizations and gifts under $1,000 lagged or declined in 2025. — [Blackbaud Institute 2025 Trends in Giving](https://institute.blackbaud.com/resources/2025-trends-in-giving); [PR Newswire release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/new-blackbaud-institute-data-shows-resilience-in-2025-charitable-giving-highlights-areas-of-opportunities-for-nonprofits-in-2026-302716900.html)
- Double the Donation (vendor; the underlying survey is not clearly named, and statistics were seen via search excerpt only):
  - 47% of nonprofits have no formal strategy for securing corporate support.
  - Almost 50% have no staff dedicated to corporate sponsorship.
  - 48% cite limited internal capacity as the biggest barrier.
  - 41% struggle with strict corporate timing and fiscal-year budget cycles.
  - 49% say finding more companies to partner with is their top corporate fundraising priority (for 2027).
  - About 1 in 3 engage sponsors less than quarterly.
  - Source: [Double the Donation: Corporate Sponsorship Statistics](https://doublethedonation.com/corporate-sponsorship-statistics/)
- 45% of associations and not-for-profits report growing difficulty securing sponsorships for conferences and events. Sponsors are shifting toward year-long partnerships. — [Bruce Rosenthal Associates survey](https://brucerosenthal.associates/articles/survey-reveals-sponsorship-challenges/)
- Forbes Nonprofit Council lists 13 challenges, including demonstrating ROI, aligning with brand goals, and reaching the right decision maker. This is opinion, from 2021. — [Forbes Nonprofit Council](https://www.forbes.com/councils/forbesnonprofitcouncil/2021/09/20/13-challenges-nonprofits-face-when-securing-corporate-sponsorships/)
- Many nonprofits "miss out on easy sponsorship dollars." Local SEO/link sponsorship is cited as an untapped channel. — [ZipSprout](https://zipsprout.com/most-nonprofits-miss-out-on-easy-sponsorship-dollars/)
- On the business side, requests usually need about 3 weeks' notice. Short notice is the top reason for decline, and personal relevance to the owner is the main filter. — [MIT Central Square case study](https://wikis.mit.edu/confluence/x/XEUkBQ)
- Corporate dollars are concentrating in top-quartile companies, which "small charities could feel." — [Chronicle of Philanthropy, 2026](https://www.philanthropy.com/news/fewer-companies-are-doing-more-of-the-giving-and-small-charities-could-feel-it/)

### Inferences
- The binding problems are discovery and access to corporate decision makers, plus timing, not only design quality. Peep's standardized one-pager helps most if it is paired with distribution (brands actively browsing) and timing signals (brand fiscal year, budget windows, tax-year deadlines).
- Since about half of nonprofits have no dedicated sponsorship staff, a free "upload any PDF, get a polished page" step removes a real labor cost. Standardization also helps brands compare causes side by side. That is the stronger argument for it.
- A 3-week lead time and personal relevance as the filter suggest brands should be able to set cause and geography preferences, and nonprofits should list needs well ahead of deadlines.

### Gaps
- No found survey data on average time to close a sponsorship, ask-to-yes success rates, or the cost of producing pitch decks. Candid, M+R Benchmarks and Chronicle sources on this were unreachable or not found.
- No data on how much time nonprofits spend chasing sponsor logos, approvals or reports. The evidence is anecdotal.
- Reddit (r/nonprofit, r/smallbusiness) and LinkedIn qualitative evidence was not collected because of the tool budget.

## 3. Brand pain points (especially SMBs) in giving and sponsoring

### Takeaway
Brands are flooded with requests (Canon Canada reports tens of thousands a year) and filter them crudely by personal relevance, lead time, or forms. Employee participation hits a ceiling (about 21-25%). Small companies largely lack the infrastructure larger firms have: only 28% of SMBs offer matching versus 65% of the Fortune 500. Corporate foundations manage more money without more staff. Low-touch, vetted, tax-clean giving is a plausible SMB need, but direct SMB survey evidence is thin.

### Cited Findings
- Inbound overload: Canon Canada says it receives "tens of thousands" of donation requests each year. — [Canon Canada sponsorship inquiry page](https://canon.ca/en/About-Canon/Donations-and-Sponsorships/Brand-Sponsorship-Inquiry)
- Retailers find donation requests "overwhelming" and dislike refusing. The recommended fix is clear guidelines plus a required online form. — [Retail Details newsletter, 2026](https://retaildetails.substack.com/p/need-help-dealing-with-donation-requests)
- Small shops are "swamped with donation requests." They filter on personal relevance and decline short-notice asks. — [MIT Central Square case study](https://wikis.mit.edu/confluence/x/XEUkBQ)
- Companies reply only when they can help, so most requests go unanswered. — [Big Joe donation FAQ](https://support.buybigjoe.com/hc/en-us/articles/51753252170388-Donation-and-Sponsorship-Request-FAQ)
- Third-party screening services such as International Sponsor Network (since 2007) exist to filter unsolicited requests for brands, which shows brands will pay to outsource vetting. — [Newswire: International Sponsor Network](https://www.newswire.com/news/international-sponsor-network-gears-up-for-expansion-158529)
- Older Australian survey (2000-01): 70% of non-donating businesses said resources were committed elsewhere, and 31% said they had never been approached (27% for sponsorship). A non-trivial share of businesses are simply never asked. — [ABS 8157.0](https://www.abs.gov.au/AUSSTATS/abs@.nsf/7d12b0f6763c78caca257061001cc588/fd2db957480ca60fca256c2100076cb7!OpenDocument)
- Lean staffing: corporate foundations are "managing more money, without more staff." — [CECP via SustainabilityHQ, 2026](https://www.sustainabilityhq.com/new-cecp-research-corporate-foundations-are-managing-more-money-without-more-staff/)
- Employee engagement plateau: on average 25% of employees volunteer and 21% use matching. — [CECP research, Aug 2026](https://www.3blmedia.com/news/new-cecp-research-corporate-employee-engagement-programs-hit-participation-ceiling-return)
- Nearly three in five employee donors say it's imperative that their employer match their donations. — [AccessNewswire release, Aug 2026](https://www.accessnewswire.com/newsroom/en/business-and-professional-services/why-your-company-should-offer-a-matching-gift-program-1200751)
- An estimated $4-7B in matching-gift funds goes unclaimed each year, a widely repeated vendor estimate. More than 26M people work for companies with matching programs. 84% of donors say they are more likely to give if a match is offered. — [Givebutter blog](https://givebutter.com/blog/matching-gifts-101-revolutionize-your-campaign-with-fundraisings-best-kept-secret); [Access Philanthropy, 2023](https://accessphilanthropy.com/what-motivates-donors-to-give/)
- Governance and political risk: about two-thirds of companies restructured purpose strategy (2025). More than three-quarters expect employee activism. Leadership churn is at 51%. — [Benevity 2025](https://benevity.com/state-of-corporate-purpose/2025)
- CSR increasingly reports to HR (33%) or Finance (18%), which suggests buyers who care about employee engagement and tax/finance efficiency respectively. — [Benevity 2025 press release](https://benevity.com/press-releases/2025-state-of-corporate-purpose)

### Inferences
- For SMBs, the job to be done is roughly: "I want to give X this year, to causes I care about, locally, with clean receipts and no ongoing admin or reputational surprises." Peep's budget-first, receipts-immediately, communication-optional model fits this. The evidence is indirect: overload, informal filters, and no staff.
- A CFO/HR reporting line means two product skins: a finance view (tax timing, receipts, a 1% floor calculator, audit trail) and an HR view (employee voting and matching).
- Vetting and reputational screening is a paid service today (ISN), so a "pre-vetted" badge has monetizable value.
- Matching-gift leakage ($4-7B unclaimed, if accurate) and low SMB match adoption (28%) point to "matching-as-a-service" for SMBs.

### Gaps
- No direct SMB survey on pain points with receipts/1099s, vetting, or proving impact. ACCP benchmarking 2025/2026 data could not be found publicly.
- The unclaimed-match figure is an unverified vendor estimate.

## 4. Cause marketing and adjacent models: what works

### Takeaway
Structured, low-friction, recurring commitments (percentage-of-revenue pledges, point-of-sale asks) move real money. 1% for the Planet certified $156M from about 4,300 members in 2025. Checkout campaigns raised $275M+ in 2024, and fixed-dollar asks beat round-ups. That validates standardized, low-effort giving mechanics and certification/badging as motivators.

### Cited Findings
- 1% for the Planet 2025 annual report: $156M certified member giving in 2025, a record. 4,298 business members, 820 of them new in 2025. $846M lifetime. — [1% for the Planet 2025 highlights](https://www.onepercentfortheplanet.org/stories/2025-annual-report-impact-highlights); [Annual reports](https://www.onepercentfortheplanet.org/annual-reports)
- Member counts conflict: about 5,200 (a 2026 profile) and 5,500 (Forbes 2023) versus 4,298 in the official report. Use the official figure. — [Forbes 2023](https://www.forbes.com/sites/afdhelaziz/2023/05/09/how-businesses-and-individuals-are-leading-the-way-with-1-for-the-planet-in-environmental-giving/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Percent_for_the_Planet)
- Engage for Good / Adyen 2025 Charity Checkout Champions (covering 2024): 92 US point-of-sale campaigns raised more than $275M. 37 campaigns (about 40%) raised more than $500K. 81% used fixed-dollar asks, which outperformed round-ups. By cause: animals about $78M, health $69M, hunger $36M. Only 74% clearly identified the benefiting charity. — [NonProfit Times](https://thenonprofittimes.com/npt_articles/checkout-campaigns-rang-up-275-million-in-2024/); [Engage for Good report](https://engageforgood.com/resource/meet-americas-charity-checkout-champions-2025/); [NonProfit PRO](https://www.nonprofitpro.com/article/2025-charity-checkout-champions-report-reveals-most-effective-campaigns/)
- Pledges tied to percentage of sales, for example Citizen's 1% of US website sales to environmental causes, show brands using giving as a marketing claim. — [Business Wire, 2020](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20201027005833/en/Citizen-Pledges-1-of-All-U.S.-Website-Sales-to-Environmental-Causes)
- Cause marketing affects consumer perception, with effects varying by fit and framing. — [MIT Sloan Management Review](https://sloanreview.mit.edu/article/how-socialcause-marketing-affects-consumer-perceptions)

### Inferences
- What works: (a) a simple, standardized commitment (a fixed percentage or a fixed dollar amount), (b) third-party certification or a badge the brand can display, and (c) cause categories with strong consumer affinity (animals, health, hunger). Peep could offer a "Peep Pledge" badge for SMBs (for example, "1% of profit, verified, receipted") as a lighter, multi-cause version of 1% for the Planet.
- The 26% of checkout campaigns that did not clearly name the charity point to a transparency gap. Verified-transfer receipts and public proof pages are a differentiator.

### Gaps
- No current data found for Sharing Excess, ShopRaise, Percent, Pledge 1% (member counts and dollars), B Corp giving requirements, or round-up apps. Not covered because of the tool budget.
- No 2024-2026 data on consumer attitudes toward cause marketing (Cone/Porter Novelli).

## 5. Evidence-backed unmet needs and feature ideas for Peep

### Takeaway
The strongest white space is SMB and mid-market giving made tax-efficient and low-touch under the new 1% floor, combined with discovery for small nonprofits who lack staff and access. The features below are mapped to the evidence above. Feature ideas are inferences.

### Inferences (feature ideas, ranked roughly by strength of evidence)
1. **1% floor optimizer and gift bunching.** Brand enters projected taxable income and entity type. Peep shows the deductible threshold, suggests a bunched amount for this tax year, and schedules disbursement over 12-24 months. This is a donor-advised-fund-like "fund now, grant later" model for SMBs; check whether that needs a DAF sponsor structure. Directly anchored in the OBBBA change.
2. **Fiscal-year and deadline calendar.** Each brand's fiscal year-end and budget window, surfaced to nonprofits, plus year-end and quarter-end "deadline giving" pushes. Addresses the 41% timing struggle.
3. **Pre-vetted nonprofit badge and reputational screen.** IRS status, a sanctions/news screen, financial health, and politically sensitive flags with filters. Monetizes what ISN-style services sell, and helps quiet-giving brands.
4. **Inbound-request redirect ("send them to Peep").** A free, embeddable "donation request" form/link that brands put on their site. Nonprofits are routed into a standardized Peep profile, which ends email overload and acquires supply.
5. **Standardized one-pager plus side-by-side compare.** Same fields across causes (cost per outcome, budget, local footprint). Standardization is the real value to brands; polish is the value to nonprofits.
6. **Visibility controls (quiet mode vs. public).** Per gift, the brand chooses anonymous, private, or public recognition, so it can give amid DEI/ESG scrutiny without publicity risk.
7. **Verified transfer and proof-of-receipt page.** A public or private link showing the funds landed, the receipt, and optional impact updates. Answers the 26% transparency gap in cause marketing.
8. **Recognition and brand-asset vault (optional).** Brand uploads logos and approved copy once; nonprofits pull approved assets without email chains. Approval workflow is built in. (Evidence is anecdotal; no survey found.)
9. **SMB matching-as-a-service and employee voting.** A small company sets a pool; employees vote on, or nominate, Peep-listed causes; Peep handles matching. Targets the 72% of SMBs without matching and the employee participation plateau.
10. **Local-first matching.** Geography filters and a "my town" feed, since 66% of SMB giving is local.
11. **Pooled giving circles for micro-brands.** Several small businesses co-fund one campaign (for example, a Main Street association) so each gets recognition at small ticket sizes.
12. **"Peep Pledge" certification badge.** A simple percentage-of-profit or fixed-dollar commitment, verified by Peep, with a badge for websites and storefronts, modeled on 1% for the Planet's traction.
13. **In-kind and cash hybrid listings.** Nonprofits list in-kind needs, with fair-market-value documentation guidance for brands.
14. **Capacity-building and general-operating option.** "Community improvement and capacity building" rose to #4 in Benevity priorities (2025), so brands can fund unrestricted needs, which deficit-strained nonprofits most need.
15. **Disaster-relief rapid pool.** Disaster giving fell 41%. A standing pool that brands opt into, auto-deployed to vetted local responders when a disaster hits.
16. **Multi-year, switchable commitments.** Given high grantee churn, renewal prompts with an easy switch, avoiding awkward breakups.
17. **Impact digest without busywork.** A once-a-year, AI-generated summary from nonprofit-supplied data, replacing custom reporting requests.

### Gaps
- Willingness to pay (brand-side fee vs. nonprofit-side fee) was not researched.
- Whether Peep holding and later disbursing funds requires a DAF or fiscal-sponsor structure, and the timing of deductions in that case, needs legal and tax review.
- Competitor landscape (Benevity, Bonterra, Percent, Goodera, Deed, Givsum, Pledge 1%, Sharing Excess, ShopRaise) is outside this note's scope.

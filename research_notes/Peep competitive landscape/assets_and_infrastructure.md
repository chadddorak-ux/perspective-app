# Peep Adjacent Landscape: (A) Brand Asset Sharing / Partner Portals and (B) Giving Infrastructure (Payments, Verification, Receipts)

Research date: 2026-10-23. US focus. Method note: WebFetch could not resolve any host during this session (DNS failures on irs.gov, every.org, pledge.to, law.cornell.edu). Everything below comes from search-result extracts of the cited pages, not full-page reads. Vendor pricing is especially volatile and often from third-party listings; each item is flagged where that applies. Nothing here is legal advice. Peep's tax and state-law structure needs review by nonprofit tax counsel.

## Q1. What exists in brand asset management and partner collaboration, and does anything already do a "public brand profile + permissioned asset vault" for grantees or sponsored partners?

### Takeaway
Plenty of tools hold brand assets (DAMs like Frontify, Brandfolder and Bynder; Canva Brand Kit; Box Hubs; Brandfetch). Plenty of tools collect sponsor logos (Cvent and Swapcard exhibitor portals, SponsorCX). I found no product where a funder or sponsor keeps one public profile plus a permissioned asset vault that every nonprofit it funds can pull from automatically. Brandfetch's free, claimable public brand profile is the closest public-profile analog. Sports-team tools like Greenfly share content in the opposite direction, from the rights-holder to the sponsor.

### Cited Findings

**Enterprise DAM and brand portals**
- **Frontify:** does not publish fixed pricing; packages are tailored per customer — [Subscribed.fyi / Frontify FAQ summary](https://subscribed.fyi/frontify/). One review lists a starter tier around $79/month, with Professional and Enterprise on request — [KI-Syndikat](https://www.ki-syndikat.de/tools/frontify/). Vendr reports a median buyer spend of about $31,739/yr, based on only 31 purchases (small sample) — [Vendr](https://www.vendr.com/marketplace/frontify).
- Frontify bills by monthly active user, and external partners who log in count toward that total. Brand guidelines, logos, colors and fonts live in one place and are released to external partners through access controls — [KI-Syndikat](https://www.ki-syndikat.de/tools/frontify/); [Rework Frontify alternatives 2026](https://resources.rework.com/tools/design-creative/best-frontify-alternatives). Implication: a per-active-user model gets expensive if hundreds of grantee nonprofits each log in.
- **Brandfolder (Smartsheet):** pricing on request. One competitor-authored comparison estimates roughly $45/user/mo (Team), $90/user/mo (Business) and custom Enterprise; it also says Brandfolder offers "custom portals for curated external sharing" and "guest access with permissions" — [Fabric comparison](https://fabric.so/comparison/brandfolder-vs-bynder). Fabric sells a competing product, so treat its figures as rough. Pricing on request is confirmed at [Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/cms/brandfolder-profile/vs/bynder/).
- **Bynder:** described as starting around $450/mo, with enterprise deals in the tens of thousands per year (competitor-authored source) — [Fabric comparison](https://fabric.so/comparison/brandfolder-vs-bynder). Strengths cited: taxonomy, permissions, portals, collections, creative workflows, approvals, analytics — [Sparkfive](https://www.sparkfive.com/blogs/brandfolder-vs-bynder-which-dam-is-right-for-you-2026).
- **Canva Brand Kit:** sharing a Brand Kit with the whole organization is limited to Canva Enterprise and Canva for Districts. Sharing with chosen teams (up to 30) is in beta for some Enterprise users, and it covers teams inside an organization, not external parties — [Canva Help](https://www.canva.com/en_gb/help/brand-kit/). Personal Brand Kits are private — [Canva Help](https://www.canva.com/en_in/help/personal-brand-kit/). Canva for Nonprofits is a free version of Pro for qualifying organizations, includes Brand Kit, and allows up to 50 users (secondary source) — [Barn2](https://barn2.com/blog/how-to-use-canva-for-nonprofits/). A third-party blog says Canva's external collaboration links often expire or require Canva accounts — [Fast.io](https://www.fast.io/resources/canva-agency-collaboration-workflow.md).
- **Dropbox:** "branded sharing" puts a company name, logo and background on externally shared links. Only admins can set it, and it does not apply to file requests. It is not an asset portal — [Dropbox](https://experience.dropbox.com/resources/how-branded-sharing-works); [Dropbox Help](https://help.dropbox.com/it-it/share/branded-sharing).
- **Box Hubs:** curated content portals that can be shared internally and externally. Available on Enterprise plans and above. Viewers need a Box account unless the Hub is shared via an open link. Box lists a company asset library as a use case — [Box Support](https://support.box.com/hc/en-us/articles/25823366568467-Creating-Deleting-and-Sharing-a-Hub).

**Logo and brand data APIs**
- **Brandfetch:** the free tier gives 100 one-time Brand API requests. The Logo API and Brand Search API are free up to 500K requests/month each with no attribution, per the official developer page — [Brandfetch developers](https://brandfetch.com/developers.md); [Brandfetch pricing](https://brandfetch.com/developers/pricing.md). Third-party sources put paid Brand API plans at about $99/mo for 2,500 fetches; PricingSaaS lists a $129/mo starter, and sources conflict — [MakerStack](https://makerstack.co/reviews/brandfetch-review/); [PricingSaaS](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/brandfetch).
- Brandfetch lets brands claim a free public brand profile, verified by a work email on the brand's domain. Verified brands can edit their page and get a badge, and hosting is "entirely free." Edits sync to "100+ partner sites" — [Brandfetch For Brands](https://brandfetch.com/why-brandfetch). I could not confirm a guidelines-upload or usage-rules feature, and Brandfetch has no notion of permissioned or private assets per recipient.

**Press kits**
- **Prezly:** reported at about $100/user/mo (Essential, includes Media Gallery) and $350/user/mo (Standard). Agency tiers run about $525–$700/mo. Figures vary by source, and FitGap cites €80/mo — [GetApp](https://www.getapp.com/it-communications-software/a/prezly/pricing/); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/143167/Prezly/pricing/); [FitGap](https://us.fitgap.com/products/prezly). Press-kit tools are public, one-to-many newsrooms with no per-partner permissions or usage-approval workflow (inferred from feature descriptions).
- I found no data on Presskit.to.

**Partner portals (B2B channel)**
- Impartner (PRM) plus Crossbeam integrate around co-marketing and co-selling, bulk deal registration and de-duplicating co-marketing lists. Impartner's marketing tools include personalized news and syndicated social — [Impartner](https://impartner.com/?p=245991); [Crossbeam Marketplace](https://marketplace.crossbeam.com/partners/impartner-prm). PartnerStack is a partnerships platform for SaaS covering recruitment, activation, tracking and commissioning — [G2](https://www.g2.com/products/crossbeam/competitors/alternatives). All of these target reseller and affiliate channels, not philanthropy.

**Sponsorship and event tools that collect logos**
- **SponsorCX:** sponsorship CRM covering sales, inventory, fulfillment, agreements and reporting. After signing, deals go into an "activation fulfillment portal" for tasks, proof of performance and artwork approvals, with the partner invited in. No public pricing — [RFP.wiki](https://www.rfp.wiki/vendors/sponsorcx). Raised $4.6M in April 2025 — [TechBuzz](https://www.techbuzznews.com/sponsorcx-raises-4-6m/). Its customers are mostly sports properties (USL, American Association of Professional Baseball) — [Sports Destinations](https://www.sportsdestinations.com/sports/soccer/sponsorcx-becomes-strategic-partner-united-soccer-34210).
- **Cvent exhibitor portal:** exhibitors upload a logo, crop it and edit company info per event — [Tango guide](https://app.tango.us/app/workflow/How-to-Add-Logo--Update-Company-Information-in-the-Cvent-Exhibitor-Portal-3ff98cf747a54773a8fdbee6c4099cd5). **Swapcard:** self-serve exhibitor portal where exhibitors register staff and manage their information — [Swapcard](https://www.swapcard.com/features/exhibitor-management-portal). Both are per-event, so the sponsor re-uploads for each event.
- **Greenfly:** a sports team or league distributes content to players and to corporate sponsors so they can post it, for example the Premier Lacrosse League giving partners content; it also tracks sponsor logo exposure (vendor claims) — [Greenfly](https://www.greenfly.com/uncategorized/sponsors-activating-partnerships-using-greenfly/); [AWS Marketplace](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/prodview-2hlwj7pt3mubo). Here assets flow from the rights-holder to the sponsor, the reverse of Peep's vault.

**Sponsorship marketplaces and prospecting tools**
- SponsorPitch helps nonprofits find companies with active philanthropic programs; SponsorUnited sells sponsorship data and benchmarking to nonprofits — [HelpYouSponsor blog 2026](https://blog.helpyousponsor.com/how-modern-nonprofits-run-sponsorship-programs-2026); [SponsorUnited](https://www.sponsorunited.com/insights/nonprofits-can-build-mission-driven-sponsorships). Sponsorfied launched a brand-sponsorship marketplace with sponsor matching done behind the scenes at first — [BetaKit](https://betakit.com/sponsorfied-launching-marketplace-for-brand-sponsorships/). None was described as moving funds, receipting or sharing assets.

### Inferences
- The "asset vault" is technically simple: object storage, a CDN, role-based access control and signed URLs, plus Brandfetch's free Logo API to pre-fill brand profiles. DAM vendors' per-user or per-MAU pricing (Frontify) would scale badly with hundreds of nonprofit viewers, so Peep should build the vault and not resell a DAM. A DAM connector (import from Frontify, Brandfolder or Bynder) would let large brands keep one source of truth.
- The gap is the access model. Access should be granted automatically by a funding relationship ("you received a grant from Brand X, so you can see Brand X's approved kit, usage rules and boilerplate for 12 months"). Usage approval should be tied to the specific campaign. None of the tools reviewed links asset access to a verified payment.
- Brandfetch is both a pre-fill source and a potential competitor for the "public brand profile," but it has no permissioning, approvals, philanthropy context or pitching.

### Gaps
- I could not open Frontify's, Bynder's or Brandfolder's own pricing and external-portal docs (fetch failures). Guest-user caps and portal pricing are unverified.
- I found no evidence either way on whether any CSR platform (Benevity, YourCause/Blackbaud, Submittable, Bonterra) offers grantees a brand-asset library. A targeted check is recommended.
- No data found on Presskit.to, PartnerStack's asset library, or SponsorCX's logo-intake specifics.

## Q2. How do events and nonprofits collect sponsor logos today, and what are the pain points?

### Takeaway
Collection is mostly manual: email and web forms with strict file specs (vector .ai/.eps/.pdf, SVG, 300 dpi). Sponsors often send unusable files, so designers re-trace logos or ship low-quality work. Event platforms make sponsors re-upload for every event.

### Cited Findings
- Novogradac accepts only .ai, .eps or .pdf vector logos with fonts converted to outlines, and reserves the right to omit non-compliant logos — [Novogradac](https://www.novoco.com/events/sponsorship-logo-requirements).
- NAB Show requests SVG or PNG for digital and EPS for print — [NAB Show](https://www.nabshow.com/resources/sponsor-logo-submission/). ISC requests vector PDF or EPS for print — [ISC](https://em.isc-hpc.com/logo.html). Pepperdine requires vector or 300 dpi (minimum 200 dpi) — [Pepperdine sponsorship packet 2025](https://bschool.pepperdine.edu/events/healthcare-symposium/content/2025-future-of-healthcare-sponsorship-packet.pdf).
- A sign-shop forum describes receiving "a scanned business card" as a logo file and re-tracing that works about 60% of the time. Shops sometimes print low-quality files as supplied because banners are short-lived (anecdotal) — [Signs101](https://signs101.com/threads/sponsorship-banners.157429).
- Event software requires a per-event logo upload and crop in an exhibitor portal (Cvent), and users ask whether square versions can be uploaded — [Tango/Cvent guide](https://app.tango.us/app/workflow/How-to-Add-Logo--Update-Company-Information-in-the-Cvent-Exhibitor-Portal-3ff98cf747a54773a8fdbee6c4099cd5); [Nebraska mailing list](https://mailman.nebraska.edu/archives/list/dwficomms@lists.nebraska.edu/thread/4QCCILBHRGFIH6G5FMN6IOS6RPNCF35E).
- Trade-press trend pieces (vendor blogs, 2026) say sponsors increasingly expect digital dashboards showing their impact — [HelpYouSponsor](https://blog.helpyousponsor.com/sponsorship-programs-need-succeed-2026).

### Inferences
- The recurring pains are: (1) wrong format or resolution, (2) no usage rules (clear space, color variants, dark or light versions), (3) the same sponsor emailed again for every event or campaign, (4) no approval trail for how the logo was used. A vault with required format slots (SVG, EPS/PDF, PNG light/dark, square/horizontal), usage rules and boilerplate fixes 1–3. A placement-proof upload ("here's how we used your logo," approve or reject) fixes 4 and doubles as proof of recognition for the 513(i) and receipting record.

### Gaps
- I found no quantified survey (hours lost, email counts) on sponsor-logo collection. Nonprofit forums such as Reddit r/nonprofit could not be fetched.

## Q3. What do the giving-infrastructure players do: disbursement, verification, fees, and who issues the receipt?

### Takeaway
Most "donation APIs" (Pledge, Every.org, Change, PayPal Giving Fund, Benevity) are run by or partner with a 501(c)(3) public charity that receives the gift, issues the tax receipt, and re-grants to the end nonprofit on a monthly-ish cycle. Fees run 0–3% on top of card processing. Verification relies on IRS data (Pub 78, BMF, auto-revocation) plus OFAC, often licensed from Candid. Chariot and Stripe are rails, not receipting intermediaries.

### Cited Findings

**Pledge (pledge.to)**
- Fees as documented by RallyUp: 3% handling for US organizations (5% international), $5 disbursement fee per beneficiary, plus 2.9% + $0.30 processing. Payout within about 15 days after each calendar month closes, by check or ACH. Campaigns using Pledge cannot use Stripe's or PayPal's nonprofit discounted rate — [RallyUp: Understand Pledge](https://rallyup.com/learn/understand-pledge/).
- Covers US registered 501(c) organizations plus vetted non-US charities. Nonprofits track donations Pledge "collects and distributes on your behalf" in the Pledge Impact Hub — [RallyUp](https://rallyup.com/learn/understand-pledge/). A GetApp listing says Pledge sends instant email tax receipts — [GetApp](https://www.getapp.com/nonprofit-software/a/pledge). Pledge's official API docs, API pricing and the identity of the receipting entity were not verified (fetch failed).

**Every.org**
- A 501(c)(3) public charity that uses a donor-advised-fund structure as the intermediary. It takes no platform fee from donors or nonprofits beyond third-party processing, and it covers processing on DAF donations — [Every.org support](https://support.every.org/hc/en-us/articles/360061886773-Does-Every-org-charge-fees); [Every.org DAF](https://www.every.org/daf-donations).
- Charity API is free for non-commercial use; for-profit use requires enterprise plans (pricing not public) — [Every.org support](https://support.every.org/hc/en-us/articles/17791468978451). Donors get a receipt right after payment and an annual consolidated receipt, with Every.org as the receipting entity — [OWID donations FAQ](https://ourworldindata.org/donations-faq). Most DAF disbursements happen within 1–3 weeks. A partner FAQ cited a 1% broker fee on stock and crypto ("currently," possibly dated) — [Every.org](https://www.every.org/daf-donations). Every.org has an API partnership with Chariot for DAF giving — [Every.org blog](https://blog.every.org/api-case-study-daf-donations-with-chariot/).

**Change (getchange.io)**
- Donations go to Our Change Foundation (OCF), a 501(c)(3), which pays beneficiaries as grants. Unregistered nonprofits get a mailed letter and must register for ACH or request a check — [Bonfire help](https://help.bonfire.com/en/articles/8813506-paying-nonprofits-via-change); [Change payouts](https://intercom.help/getchange/en/articles/10029655-payouts).
- G2 describes Change's approach as "DAF-first," so companies can donate to many nonprofits without extra administrative or legal burden — [G2](https://www.g2.com/sellers/get-change).
- International grants route through GlobalGiving at a 15% corporate fee plus a $750–900 vetting fee unless a direct agreement exists — [Change help](https://intercom.help/getchange/en/articles/12675213-international-donations-globalgiving).
- Shopify app Basic plan is $25/mo — [Shopify](https://apps.shopify.com/getchange?locale=es). API fee levels were not found.

**PayPal Giving Fund (PPGF)**
- PPGF, or partners on its behalf, issues the official receipts, so donors get a receipt from PayPal and not the charity — [PayPal Help](https://paypal.com/bh/cshelp/article/when-and-how-does-paypal-giving-fund-grant-donations-to-charities-help969); [Donorbox](https://donorbox.org/nonprofit-blog/?p=97945).
- Enrolled charities are granted within 15–45 days; unenrolled charities get a check within 90 days. The Donation Delivery Policy (last updated March 2022, dated) says grants go out around the 25th monthly — [PayPal Help](https://paypal.com/bh/cshelp/article/when-and-how-does-paypal-giving-fund-grant-donations-to-charities-help969); [PPGF Delivery Policy](https://paypal.com/webapps/mpp/givingfund/policies/donation-delivery-policy).
- Sources conflict on fees: Donorbox and ShoppingGives say no fees to charities, while PYMNTS says a small admin fee is deducted.

**Chariot (DAFpay)**
- DAF-grant initiation rail that connects donors to their existing DAF sponsors. A typical 2.9% initiation fee can be absorbed by the donor or the nonprofit, and platforms may stack fees on top — [Chariot help](https://help.givechariot.com/dafpay/dafpay-faqs); [Kind Kiosk](https://guides.kindkiosk.com/article/processing-fees-explained-revised). Fees are deducted before payout or invoiced under contract; direct subscriptions also exist — [Chariot docs](https://docs.givechariot.com/guides/dafpay/faq). The receipt comes from the donor's DAF sponsor (inference from the DAF structure; not stated in the results).

**Stripe / Stripe Connect**
- Connect, where the platform handles pricing: $2 per active account per month (active means a payout occurred that month) plus 0.25% + $0.25 per payout. Instant payouts cost 1%. Card processing starts at 2.9% + $0.30 — [Stripe Connect pricing](https://stripe.com/fr-us/connect/pricing); [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/business/software/learn/stripe-fees).
- Nonprofit discount: widely reported as 2.2% + $0.30 for 501(c)(3)s with more than 80% of volume from tax-deductible donations. It requires an application, is not retroactive, and excludes Amex and tickets. Stripe does not publish the rate — [Givebutter](https://givebutter.com/blog/stripe-for-nonprofits); [Harness](https://help.goharness.com/en/articles/3159903-nonprofit-discount-for-donation-processing-on-stripe-2-2-0-30); [Kind Kiosk](https://guides.kindkiosk.com/article/stripe-nonprofit-rate). The discount applies to the charity's own account, so a for-profit platform account would not qualify (inference).

**Benevity (Causes Portal)**
- Benevity's foundation partners charge an admin fee of up to 2.9%, netted from the donation. Check payouts carry a 7% fee (minimum $25, maximum $100) after a charity's third check. Donations are pooled monthly, and 89% go out by EFT in the US and Canada — [Benevity Causes help](https://causeshelp.benevity.org/hc/articles/360001149526-How-to-sign-up-for-PayPal-payments); [Benevity client help pages](https://cenovus.benevity.org/community/help).
- A nonprofit must exist in the Causes Portal to receive funds and goes through vetting and validation. PayPal payout requires an email on the organization's own domain — [Benevity B-Hive](https://b-hive.benevity.com/hc/articles/360045765091). A public "Causes API" for third parties was not found.

**Verification data and APIs**
- **IRS TEOS bulk downloads:** Pub 78, the Automatic Revocation list, 990-N and 990-series data, all free and updated monthly (the cached page showed last update December 12, 2025). Caveats: DBA names are not in Pub 78, and revoked organizations may since have been reinstated — [IRS TEOS bulk data](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/tax-exempt-organization-search-bulk-data-downloads); [IRS TEOS](https://www.irs.gov/teos).
- **Candid published starting prices:** Charity Check API from $2,750; Essentials API from $4,800 (1.6M+ nonprofits, 40+ fields); Premier API from $9,900 (760+ more fields, 990 PDFs); 30-day free trial — [Candid explore APIs](https://candid.org/explore-apis/).
- Charity Check validates by EIN against Pub 78, the BMF, the Internal Revenue Bulletin, auto-revocation and the OFAC SDN list, with time-stamped audit documentation. A bulk endpoint exists, pricing not found — [Jentic listing](https://jentic.com/apis/candid.org/candid); [Candid developer](https://developer.candid.org/reference/v4).
- **Percent, now Goodstack:** API-first nonprofit database plus compliance and payments layer. It validates against official registries in each country plus due-diligence screening, and claims 9M+ causes. Pricing not found — [Percent docs](https://percent.super.site/nonprofit-validation-overview); [Goodstack](https://goodstack.io/verifications).
- I found no current data on Cybergrants' verification service.

### Inferences
- There are two receipting models:
  - **Intermediary / DAF model** (Every.org, Pledge, Change/OCF, PPGF, Benevity's foundations): the platform's charity receives the gift and issues the receipt, and the end nonprofit receives a grant. This gives one receipt, simple compliance, and the ability to fund any verified 501(c)(3) without onboarding it. The trade-off is variance power, and the end nonprofit is not the receipting entity.
  - **Direct model** (Stripe Connect into the nonprofit's own account): the nonprofit receives the funds and issues its own receipt. Peep would then need every nonprofit onboarded (KYC) and would be a facilitator.
- For sponsorship with recognition benefits, the intermediary/DAF model collides with the IRC 4967 more-than-incidental-benefit rule (see Q6). Peep may need a hybrid: pure gifts through an intermediary, and sponsorships paid directly to the nonprofit (Stripe Connect), with the nonprofit issuing the acknowledgment.

### Gaps
- I could not verify Pledge API pricing, the Every.org commercial tier, Change API fees or Goodstack pricing from primary pages.
- PPGF fees are contested between sources.
- It is unclear whether Benevity exposes its Causes database through a licensable API.

## Q4. Which components could Peep license, and which must it build?

### Takeaway
License the commodity layers: payment rails (Stripe Connect), nonprofit verification (IRS bulk files free, with Candid Charity Check or Goodstack for audit-grade checks), logo pre-fill (Brandfetch), and possibly a fiscal intermediary for pure gifts (Every.org, Pledge or Change). Build the differentiators: standardized one-pagers, matching, the permissioned brand vault with usage approvals, sponsorship-vs-gift classification, and the receipt/acknowledgment generator.

### Cited Findings
- Stripe Connect handles multi-party payouts at $2 per active account per month plus 0.25% + $0.25 per payout — [Stripe Connect pricing](https://stripe.com/fr-us/connect/pricing).
- IRS Pub 78, BMF and auto-revocation files are free, monthly bulk downloads — [IRS TEOS bulk data](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/tax-exempt-organization-search-bulk-data-downloads).
- Candid Charity Check starts at $2,750 and adds OFAC screening plus time-stamped audit trails — [Candid](https://candid.org/explore-apis/).
- Brandfetch Logo and Search APIs are free up to 500K requests/mo, and brands can claim profiles — [Brandfetch](https://brandfetch.com/developers.md); [Brandfetch For Brands](https://brandfetch.com/why-brandfetch).
- Every.org's Charity API is free for non-commercial use and requires an enterprise plan for for-profits — [Every.org support](https://support.every.org/hc/en-us/articles/17791468978451).
- Change's "DAF-first" model lets companies fund many nonprofits without per-nonprofit administrative burden — [G2](https://www.g2.com/sellers/get-change).
- Pledge charges 3% plus $5 per beneficiary plus processing — [RallyUp](https://rallyup.com/learn/understand-pledge/).

### Inferences
- **Payments:** Stripe Connect is the default. Unverified nonprofits can be paid by check or ACH through an intermediary partner, as Change and PPGF do.
- **Verification:** ingest the IRS files nightly (free). Add Candid Charity Check at payout time for an auditable, OFAC-screened snapshot. Goodstack is an option if international scope matters later.
- **Receipts:** must build or own, because receipt content depends on the gift-vs-sponsorship classification and the benefits delivered. For pure gifts, partnering with a DAF intermediary is fastest to market but hands receipting (and brand ownership of the receipt) to that partner.
- **Proof of delivery:** "Peep verifies funds reached the nonprofit" can come from Stripe payout confirmations or a partner's grant confirmation, plus the nonprofit's acknowledgment. This must be built on top of the rails.
- **Licensing gotcha:** the Every.org API commercial terms and Pledge API terms must be negotiated, since Peep is for-profit. Stacking an intermediary fee (about 3%) on Peep's own take raises the total cost.

### Gaps
- Exact enterprise API terms for Every.org, Pledge and Change were not found.

## Q5. What are realistic fee levels and receipting models?

### Takeaway
All-in payment plus intermediary costs typically land between about 2.2% and 6% of the gift. Platform-specific take rates sit on top. Receipting is done either by an intermediary 501(c)(3) (most APIs) or by the end nonprofit (direct rails).

### Cited Findings
- Card processing is 2.9% + $0.30 standard, or 2.2% + $0.30 at the nonprofit rate where the charity is the merchant — [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/business/software/learn/stripe-fees); [Givebutter](https://givebutter.com/blog/stripe-for-nonprofits).
- Pledge: 3% handling (5% international) plus $5 per beneficiary plus 2.9% + $0.30 — [RallyUp](https://rallyup.com/learn/understand-pledge/).
- Every.org: 0% platform fee, processing passed through, DAF processing covered — [Every.org](https://support.every.org/hc/en-us/articles/360061886773-Does-Every-org-charge-fees).
- Benevity: up to 2.9% admin fee; check fee of 7% (minimum $25, maximum $100) after three checks — [Benevity help](https://cenovus.benevity.org/community/help).
- Chariot DAFpay: typically a 2.9% initiation fee — [Chariot help](https://help.givechariot.com/dafpay/dafpay-faqs).
- GlobalGiving route via Change: 15% corporate fee for international grants — [Change help](https://intercom.help/getchange/en/articles/12675213-international-donations-globalgiving).
- Pledge It (a different company) charges a 5.9% platform fee plus Stripe — [Pledge It support](https://support.pledgeit.org/en/articles/10433150-fee-settings).
- Payout timing benchmarks: Pledge pays within 15 days after month-end; PPGF in 15–45 days; Every.org DAF in 1–3 weeks; Benevity monthly — sources above.

### Inferences
- For brand budgets, which are large and ACH-capable, Peep should push ACH or wire funding. That avoids card fees (2–3%) and leaves room for a transparent platform fee charged to the brand on top of the gift, not deducted from it. Nonprofits are fee-sensitive and Every.org has set a 0% benchmark.
- **California AB 488 implication:** if Peep is a "charitable fundraising platform," it must register with the CA AG (required since June 12, 2024), hold donations in a separate account, give conspicuous disclosures of maximum time to disbursement, and account for fees — [CA OAG](https://oag.ca.gov/node/584000); [Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2021/10/california-enacts-law-regulating-charitable-fund). Published payout SLAs and a fee breakdown are therefore both a compliance need and a selling point.

### Gaps
- Whether AB 488 applies to B2B brand-to-nonprofit matching (as opposed to public solicitation) needs counsel review.
- I found no benchmark data on take rates charged by B2B sponsorship marketplaces.

## Q6. What legal pitfalls apply to "sponsorship with benefits," and how should Peep frame tiers and recognition?

### Takeaway
Three different regimes apply:
1. **For the nonprofit:** whether the payment is a non-taxable qualified sponsorship payment under IRC 513(i) or taxable advertising income (UBIT).
2. **For the brand:** whether the payment is a charitable contribution (IRC 170, now subject to a 1% floor and 10% cap for corporations from 2026) or a business expense (IRC 162).
3. **For donors and receipts:** substantiation and quid pro quo disclosure.

Peep should limit recognition to "acknowledgment" (name, logo, value-neutral tagline, link). It should avoid exclusivity-of-provider deals, calls to action and endorsements. It should not route benefit-bearing sponsorships through a DAF intermediary.

### Cited Findings

**IRC 513(i) / Treas. Reg. 1.513-4 (nonprofit side)**
- A qualified sponsorship payment carries no arrangement or expectation of a substantial return benefit beyond use or acknowledgment of the sponsor's name, logo or product lines. Acknowledgment excludes advertising: qualitative or comparative language, price or value information, endorsements, or inducements to buy — [IRS: Advertising or QSP?](https://www.irs.gov/node/13726); [Federal Register TD 2002](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2002-04-25/pdf/02-9930.pdf).
- **2% de minimis rule:** benefits are disregarded if their aggregate fair market value is 2% or less of the payment. If they exceed 2%, the entire value counts as a return benefit, not just the excess — [federal-regs.com 1.513-4](https://federal-regs.com/title/26/part-1/1.513-4/). CapinCrouse example: benefits over 2% of a $5,000 payment meant the whole $400 benefit was treated as advertising subject to UBIT — [CapinCrouse](https://capincrouse.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/CapinCrouse-What-Nonprofits-Need-to-Know-About-Corporate-Sponsorships.pdf).
- **Exclusive sponsor vs exclusive provider:** being named "exclusive sponsor" alone is not a substantial return benefit. An exclusive provider arrangement that restricts competing products is, so only the excess over its value qualifies — [IRS: Exclusive provider](https://www.irs.gov/node/44166); [federal-regs.com](https://federal-regs.com/title/26/part-1/1.513-4/).
- **Bifurcation:** if part of a payment buys advertising, that portion is taxable and the remainder can still be a QSP — [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/node/13726).
- FMV is measured as of the contract date for binding written contracts — [federal-regs.com](https://federal-regs.com/title/26/part-1/1.513-4/).

**Substantiation and quid pro quo (IRS Pub 1771)**
- Donors need a contemporaneous written acknowledgment for gifts of $250 or more. It must name the organization, state the amount, and state whether goods or services were provided, with a description and good-faith value estimate if so — [IRS: Written acknowledgments](https://www.irs.gov/Charities-&-Non-Profits/Charitable-Organizations/Charitable-Contributions-Written-Acknowledgments).
- Charities must give a disclosure statement for quid pro quo payments over $75. The threshold applies to the total payment, and separate payments are not aggregated. Token items are excepted. The penalty is $10 per noncompliant disclosure, capped at $5,000 per event or mailing — [IRS Pub 1771 page](https://www.irs.gov/node/551); [MSK alert](https://www.msk.com/newsroom-alerts-1839).

**Brand side: IRC 170 vs 162 and the 2026 corporate 1% floor**
- Under the OBBBA, from tax years starting January 1, 2026, corporate charitable deductions are allowed only above 1% of taxable income and are still capped at 10%. Amounts disallowed by the floor can carry forward only when gifts exceed the 10% cap — [Pierce Atwood](https://www.pierceatwood.com/alerts/congress-imposes-1-floor-corporate-charitable-deductions); [Greenberg Traurig](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2025/10/new-limitations-on-charitable-deductions-take-effect-in-2026).
- A payment cannot be deducted under both 162 and 170. Section 162 requires a direct relationship to the business and a reasonable expectation of commensurate financial return, and should be documented at the time of payment. Section 162 expenses are fully deductible with no floor or cap. Sponsorships of galas and golf events with expected advertising or goodwill are commonly deducted as business expenses — [Bloomberg Tax: "New Corporate Charitable Donation Rule Is a Double-Edged Sword"](https://news.bloombergtax.com/daily-tax-report/new-corporate-charitable-donation-rule-is-a-double-edged-sword); [Keiter CPA](https://keitercpa.com/?p=6343).

**DAF intermediaries and sponsorship benefits (IRC 4967)**
- IRC 4967 imposes a 125% excise tax on a donor or advisor who receives a more-than-incidental benefit from a DAF distribution — [GovInfo 26 USC 4967](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2021-title26-subtitleD-chap42-subchapG-sec4967.htm).
- IRS Notice 2017-73 (proposed approach; I could not confirm final regulations) treats a DAF grant that lets the donor attend a charity event as a prohibited benefit, even if the donor pays the non-deductible portion separately — [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/newsroom/alerts/2017/12/IRS-Notice-Provides-Guidance-on-use-of-Donor-Advised-Funds-for-Pledges-and-Charity-Events).
- No safe-harbor threshold exists, and prohibited benefits must be returned — [Council on Foundations comments](https://cof.org/sites/default/files/documents/files/FINALCommentsDAFNotice2017-73.pdf); [Nonprofit Law Blog](https://nonprofitlawblog.com/donor-advised-fund-regulations-request-comments/).
- AAFRC: benefits beyond token are prohibited for DAF grants — [AAFRC](https://aafrc.org/can-a-donor-receive-event-benefits-for-a-daf-grant/).

**Commercial co-venturer (CCV) laws**
- About 24 states plus DC regulate CCVs (compliance-vendor estimate), and requirements vary:
  - Alabama: AG registration ($100/yr, expires September 30), surety bond, and closing statement within 90 days.
  - Massachusetts: registration, bond and contract filing.
  - Connecticut: agreement filed 10 days before the promotion, no bond.
  - Oregon and Arizona: no registration.
  - Some states may treat cause promotions as professional fundraising depending on wording.
  - Source: [Labyrinth Inc state guides](https://labyrinthinc.com/cause-marketing-commercial-co-venture-guide-alabama/) (single compliance-vendor source; verify against state AG sites).
- CCV rules are triggered when a business represents to the public that a purchase will benefit a charity (cause marketing).

**Platform regulation: California AB 488 (Gov. Code 12599.9)**
- Platforms must register with the CA AG Registry (since June 12, 2024) and file regular reports. They must provide conspicuous disclosures (such as maximum time to grant), obtain charity consent to use the charity's name (with exceptions), hold funds in a separate account, and account for fees. Commercial platforms carry additional disclosures — [CA OAG](https://oag.ca.gov/node/584000); [Venable 2021](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2021/10/california-enacts-law-regulating-charitable-fund); [Venable 2022 rulemaking](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2022/06/california-issues-notice-of-proposed-rulemaking).

### Inferences
- **Frame Peep tiers as acknowledgment levels, not ad packages.**
  - Allowed: logo size and placement on the nonprofit's campaign page or report, "Supported by [Brand]" with a link, a value-neutral brand tagline, and listing in an annual report.
  - Avoid inside a "gift" tier: endorsements, calls to action ("Shop Brand X"), discount codes, comparative claims, exclusive-provider promises, employee event tickets and content deliverables.
  - Anything beyond acknowledgment belongs in a separate "marketing services" or "advertising" line, priced and invoiced separately. The brand can treat it as a §162 expense, and the nonprofit reports it as potential UBI.
- **Default receipt for a QSP:** "[Brand] received no goods or services other than use or acknowledgment of its name/logo" — but see Gaps on whether logo acknowledgment is a "good or service" for §170(f)(8) purposes. Where tickets or tables are included, the receipt must state the good-faith FMV, and Peep should auto-check the 2% de minimis rule.
- **The 1% floor changes brand incentives.** Many corporations will prefer §162 treatment for sponsorships. Peep could offer brands a choice of "gift" (charitable receipt, recognition limited to acknowledgment) or "sponsorship" (business-expense invoice from the nonprofit, broader recognition, possible UBIT for the nonprofit on any advertising portion). It should document the business purpose at the time of payment.
- **Do not route recognition-bearing sponsorships through a DAF intermediary account the brand advises.** Under 4967 the brand, as donor-advisor, risks a 125% excise tax for more-than-incidental benefits such as event tickets, and possibly ad-like recognition. Pure gifts with name-only acknowledgment through a DAF are generally lower risk, but counsel should confirm. If brands fund through their own corporate DAF, Peep must strip benefits.
- **CCV exposure:** if brands publicize "buy X and we donate to Y" through Peep, the brand (and possibly Peep) can trigger CCV registration and bonding in regulated states. Peep should frame its product as fixed, pre-committed giving budgets, not per-sale promotions, or provide CCV compliance tooling.
- **AB 488:** if Peep receives or directs funds and solicits on behalf of charities, CA registration, disclosures and a separate trust account are likely required. Peep should build to that standard nationally.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch Pub 1771's or Pub 598's full text. Whether a sponsor's logo acknowledgment counts as "goods or services" that reduce the §170 deduction, and what exact receipt language applies to QSPs, need a primary-source or counsel check.
- I found no source confirming whether Notice 2017-73's DAF event-benefit approach was finalized in DAF regulations; the proposed DAF regulations status as of 2026 is unverified.
- No consolidated, authoritative 50-state CCV table was found from state AG sources, only a compliance vendor's.
- Whether a B2B matching platform falls under AB 488's definition of "charitable fundraising platform" and whether Peep would be a "commercial" platform needs legal analysis.

## Q7. Does anything resembling the brand asset vault exist for sponsorships or CSR, and what would make Peep's unique?

### Takeaway
Pieces exist separately: a public brand profile (Brandfetch), DAM external portals (Frontify, Brandfolder, Bynder, Box Hubs), per-event logo intake (Cvent, Swapcard), sponsor fulfillment and artwork approval (SponsorCX), rights-holder-to-sponsor content sharing (Greenfly), and donation rails with receipting (Every.org, Pledge, Change). No product found combines a funder-owned brand profile, a vault unlocked by verified funding, usage-rule approvals, a pitch inbox, and receipting that reflects recognition benefits.

### Cited Findings
- Brandfetch offers free claimable public profiles that sync to 100+ partner sites, with no permissioning — [Brandfetch](https://brandfetch.com/why-brandfetch).
- Frontify and Brandfolder release assets to external partners through access controls but charge per active user or seat — [KI-Syndikat](https://www.ki-syndikat.de/tools/frontify/); [Fabric](https://fabric.so/comparison/brandfolder-vs-bynder).
- SponsorCX's fulfillment portal includes artwork approvals and proof of performance with partners invited in, aimed at sports properties — [RFP.wiki](https://www.rfp.wiki/vendors/sponsorcx).
- Greenfly sends rights-holder content to sponsors and tracks logo exposure — [Greenfly](https://www.greenfly.com/uncategorized/sponsors-activating-partnerships-using-greenfly/).
- Cvent and Swapcard require per-event exhibitor or sponsor uploads — [Tango/Cvent](https://app.tango.us/app/workflow/How-to-Add-Logo--Update-Company-Information-in-the-Cvent-Exhibitor-Portal-3ff98cf747a54773a8fdbee6c4099cd5); [Swapcard](https://www.swapcard.com/features/exhibitor-management-portal).
- Nonprofit-side prospecting tools (SponsorPitch, SponsorUnited) find sponsors but do not move money or share assets — [HelpYouSponsor](https://blog.helpyousponsor.com/how-modern-nonprofits-run-sponsorship-programs-2026).

### Inferences
Peep's possible differentiators:
1. **Access gated by verified funding.** Asset rights are granted automatically when a payout clears and expire on a schedule, and they are revocable.
2. **Usage rules and compliance built in.** Required file slots (SVG, EPS, PNG light/dark), clear-space and color rules, approved boilerplate, plus guardrails that keep recognition within 513(i) "acknowledgment" (for example, no taglines with qualitative claims in gift tiers).
3. **Placement proof loop.** The nonprofit uploads its usage, the brand approves it, and that becomes both proof of performance for the brand and documentation for the acknowledgment and receipt record.
4. **Receipt generator tied to benefits.** It produces the right document (gift acknowledgment with a goods/services statement, a QSP acknowledgment, or a sponsorship invoice) and applies the 2% test and the $75 quid pro quo test automatically.
5. **Pitch inbox on the brand profile with standardized one-pagers.** Brand-side intake replaces cold email.
6. **Free pre-fill** from Brandfetch's Logo API, plus a possible importer from enterprise DAMs.

The defensible moat is combining money movement and receipting with the asset relationship. Storage alone is not defensible: Brandfetch or a DAM could add public kits, but they lack the payments, verification and tax layer.

### Gaps
- I did not confirm whether Benevity, YourCause/Blackbaud or Bonterra offer grantee brand-kit features. A targeted check of their grantee portals is recommended before claiming "first."
- I found no evidence of brand willingness to pay for a vault specifically. Demand signals are inferred from logo-collection pain and DAM adoption.

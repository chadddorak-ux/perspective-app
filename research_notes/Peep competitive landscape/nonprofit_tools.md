# Nonprofit-Side Fundraising, Campaign-Page and Funder-Discovery Tools (as of 2026-10-23)

Scope note: US focus. Pricing is volatile and many third-party pricing pages contradict each other; where a figure comes from a vendor's own page fetched during this research (Oct 2026) it is marked "(vendor page, Oct 2026)". Competitor-authored comparisons (Zeffy, Donorbox, Granted AI, Grantable, PayIt2) are flagged as such. Several primary sites (candid.org, every.org pricing, gofundme.com pricing, businesswire) blocked automated fetches; those figures are from search snippets or secondary sources and are marked.

## 1. What does each tool do and charge? (player profiles)

### Takeaway
The market splits into (a) grant/funder-discovery subscriptions ($0-$1,159/mo; Instrumentl, Candid, Grantable, GrantStation) that focus almost entirely on foundations and government grants, and (b) donation/campaign-page platforms that have raced to $0 platform fees funded by donor "tips" (Zeffy, Givebutter, Every.org) or charge 1.6-3.95% platform fees plus subscriptions (Donorbox, GoFundMe Pro). None of them brings corporate or brand money to the nonprofit; they are tools for converting donors the nonprofit already has, or for finding foundation grants.

### Inferences
- Platform-fee pricing for small-dollar nonprofit fundraising has collapsed toward $0 (Zeffy, Givebutter with tips, Every.org, PayPal Giving Fund, GoFundMe 0% + processing). A Peep model that charges nonprofits a platform fee would be swimming against the market; charging the funding side (brands) or monetizing the transfer/receipt/verification service fits better.
- Funder-discovery pricing is bifurcating: Candid search is now free-to-search, Grantable has a free tier, while Instrumentl moved upmarket ($299-$1,159/mo). Small nonprofits are being pushed toward free-but-capped or AI-assisted tools.
- Kickstarter-style tiered rewards are the best UX analog for "giving tiers", but nonprofit platforms mostly offer only suggested-amount grids, not packaged sponsorship tiers with deliverables.

## 2. Do any help nonprofits find CORPORATE sponsors (not just foundations/individuals)? How good is it?

### Takeaway
Corporate-sponsor discovery for small nonprofits is the weakest-served part of the stack. Grant databases cover corporate foundations (990-PF filers) but not corporate marketing/CSR budgets; the few sponsor-specific tools are either a research database (Double the Donation), an SEO-link brokerage that has placed under $10M in a decade (ZipSprout), or sports/event sponsorship marketplaces. Practitioner guides openly say "there is no big corporate sponsorship directory."

### Cited Findings
- Corporate giving was $43.67B in 2025 (+3.1%, ~7% of $617.2B total US giving), slowest-growing source in 2025 but +60% over five years vs +29% for total giving (Giving USA 2026). — [Giving USA](https://givingusa.org/giving-usa-charitable-giving-rose-to-617-20-billion-in-2025-surpassing-the-600-billion-mark-for-the-first-time/); [Ohio CPA summary](https://ohiocpa.com/search/utilities/displaynewsitem/2026/07/10/key-takeaways-from-giving-usa-on-2025-philanthropy-trends)
- Business giving is hard to measure well (Lilly School methodology note). — [Candid blog](https://candid.org/blogs/philanthropy-trends-giving-usa/)
- NeonOne: "there is no big corporate sponsorship directory," so the only way to know whether a company fits is by asking; advice is to target companies sharing your audience and bring specific partnership ideas. — [Neon One](https://neonone.com/resources/blog/corporate-sponsorships-for-nonprofits/)
- Zeffy guide: build relationships 3-6 months before asking; present 3-4 clear tiers with quantified benefits; don't cold-email a 200-company generic list. — [Zeffy blog](https://zeffy.com/blog/how-to-get-corporate-sponsors-for-nonprofits)
- Double the Donation sponsorship database: 24,000+ companies with application portals, sponsorship guidelines, focus areas, typical donation amounts, regional preferences, plus matching gifts/volunteer grants/corporate grants (vendor claims). Pricing for the sponsorship module not published; matching-gift product ranges from ~$999/yr standard to $30,000/yr enterprise (aggregators). — [Double the Donation](https://doublethedonation.com/sponsorships-demo/); [SoftwareFinder](https://softwarefinder.com/nonprofit/double-the-donation); [GetApp](https://www.getapp.sg/software/2083096/double-the-donation)
- ZipSprout: free nonprofit registration; hand-matches nonprofits/events with businesses; $9,778,328 sponsorship dollars placed and 29,904 sponsorships since Jan 2016; 167,442 opportunities across 710 locations; model is sponsor logo/link placement on nonprofit websites (local SEO). — [ZipSprout](https://zipsprout.com/nonprofits-events-organization-sign-up/)
- SponsorPitch: sales-prospecting data on 13,000+ brands' sponsorships and decision-maker contacts; SponsorFlo marketplace (no listing/success fees); Anvara (sports/festivals sponsorship marketplace); SponsorMatch (AI sponsor suggestions with deal pricing and pitch angles). — [SponsorPitch](https://sponsorpitch.com/); [SponsorFlo](https://app.sponsorflo.ai/marketplace); [Anvara](https://www.anvara.com/); [SponsorMatch](https://sponsormatch.pro/)
- Instrumentl Pre-Award "Peer prospecting" shows "which funders support organizations like yours" (funders from 990 data — foundations incl. corporate foundations, not CSR/marketing budgets). — [Instrumentl pricing](https://www.instrumentl.com/pricing)
- Kindsight's Grant Writer targets foundation, corporate and government applications (application drafting, not discovery). — search summary of [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/investor/iwave-information-systems)
- OneCause includes "Online Sponsorship Sales" and "Sponsor Management" for events — i.e., managing sponsors a nonprofit already has. — [OneCause pricing](https://www.onecause.com/pricing/)
- Benevity (corporate workplace-giving platform) requires nonprofits to register in its Causes Portal (legal name, registration number, letter of authorization, banking info), with statuses "Verified", "Self-certified" (may be required to apply for grants) and "Tax eligible"; this is how nonprofits become eligible for employee giving/matching at Benevity client companies — passive eligibility, not active matching. — [Benevity Causes help](https://causeshelp.benevity.org/hc/articles/360001158326); [Benevity status labels (via search)](https://b-hive.benevity.com/hc/articles/360045765091)
- Double the Donation (partner) says most eligible donors never submit a matching-gift request. — [Double the Donation / Bright Funds](https://doublethedonation.com/bright-funds/)

### Inferences
- ZipSprout's numbers imply an average of ~$327 per sponsorship ($9.78M / 29,904), showing that existing "brand-to-nonprofit" matching is small-ticket and SEO-motivated, not budgeted CSR giving. This is a clear white space for Peep: brands with set giving budgets matched to standardized campaign one-pagers.
- Small nonprofits' corporate money today comes through (1) personal network/board, (2) local business asks, (3) passive eligibility in employee-giving platforms (Benevity etc.), and (4) corporate foundation grants found via grant databases. None is a marketplace where brands browse and fund.
- Corporate giving's 5-year growth (+60%) outpacing total giving suggests budget exists, but measurement is opaque — a platform that gives brands verified, receipted, reportable placements could address the brand side's reporting needs.

### Gaps
- No reliable data found on what share of small (<$1M) nonprofits receive any corporate funding.
- No r/nonprofit threads surfaced via search on corporate sponsor discovery; Reddit-specific sentiment on this topic is unverified.
- Could not find pricing/adoption data for Double the Donation's sponsorship module specifically.

## 3. Do any turn existing documents (PDF decks) into campaign pages? What AI features exist in 2025-2026? How are campaigns presented?

### Takeaway
No tool found converts an uploaded PDF/pitch deck into a campaign page. The closest is Givebutter Magic's "Campaign creator", which turns a nonprofit's website into a first-draft campaign (story, branding, images). Grant tools (Grantable, Instrumentl Apply) ingest documents but output proposals, not public pages. AI in donation platforms is concentrated on copy suggestions, ask-amount optimization and goals; presentation is template-driven and largely self-serve, so design quality depends on the nonprofit's own effort.

### Cited Findings

## 4. How is nonprofit verification done, and how are tax receipts handled?

### Takeaway
Verification is mostly automated against IRS data (Pub 78 / Business Master File), sometimes routed through an intermediary charity (PayPal Giving Fund, Every.org) that legally receives the gift, re-grants it, and issues the tax receipt. Direct processors (Givebutter, Zeffy, Donorbox, GoFundMe Pro) have the nonprofit as merchant of record, with the platform auto-sending receipts on the nonprofit's behalf. Corporate platforms (Benevity) add manual vetting and self-certification layers.

### Cited Findings
- Donors and grantmakers may rely on IRS Publication 78 (electronic-only) or the IRS Business Master File; BMF carries subsection/509(a) classification, Pub 78 does not; DBA names are not listed in Pub 78. — [Mitchell Silberberg & Knupp](https://msk.com/newsroom-alerts-1739)
- California law requires charitable fundraising platforms operating in California to solicit only for nonprofits in good standing with the state AG, Franchise Tax Board and IRS. — search summary of [MSK alert](https://msk.com/newsroom-alerts-1739) (not independently verified from statute)
- PayPal Giving Fund: donation is a final gift to PayPal Giving Fund (a DAF-structured charity) which re-grants to the chosen charity; PPGF issues the donor's receipt; PPGF charges no fees to donors or charities, but partners may deduct processing/platform fees; donor data is shared only with charities enrolled with PPGF. — [PayPal Giving Fund donor terms](https://www.paypal.com/us/webapps/mpp/givingfund/policies/donor-terms-of-service)
- Every.org sends the donor's official tax receipt (including for crypto, which it liquidates and disburses); disburses via Stripe Connect, PayPal Giving Fund or For Good. — [Every.org help center search](https://support.every.org/hc/en-us/search?query=tax+receipt); [Every.org blog](https://blog.every.org/how-does-every-org-work-your-top-10-pricing-questions-answered/)
- GoFundMe Pro keeps white-label tools "so donors receive tax receipts and the owned experience from the nonprofit organization." Pro now offers donor self-serve tax summaries and offline-donation receipts. — [NonProfit Times](https://thenonprofittimes.com/npt_articles/gofundme-pro-launching-retiring-classy-name/); [GoFundMe Pro blog](https://pro.gofundme.com/c/blog/new-on-gofundme-pro-h1-2026/)
- GoFundMe built its 1.4M nonprofit pages from "publicly available data" on IRS-recognized 501(c)(3)s plus third-party sources; claiming a page requires searching by name/EIN and accepting GoFundMe terms; fiscally sponsored groups struggled to claim. — [Chronicle of Philanthropy](https://www.philanthropy.com/news/backlash-grows-as-charities-say-gofundmes-donation-pages-create-chaos/)
- Donorbox Pro offers "receipt whitelabeling" (implying platform-generated receipts on the nonprofit's behalf). — [Donorbox pricing](https://donorbox.org/pricing)
- Zeffy checks the organization's official nonprofit registration during onboarding. — search summary of [Zeffy donate pages](https://www.zeffy.com/donate/new-york)
- Candid Seals of Transparency (Bronze-Platinum) are used as a trust signal; Candid ties its free "Go for Gold" Premium year to earning a 2026 Gold Seal. — [GrantCue citing Candid Learning](https://www.grantcue.com/blog/is-foundation-directory-being-discontinued)
- Benevity: five-step registration with letter of authorization, banking info, government registration number; manual review; statuses Verified / Self-certified / Tax eligible. — [Benevity Causes help](https://causeshelp.benevity.org/hc/articles/360001158326); [Benevity status labels](https://b-hive.benevity.com/hc/articles/360045765091)
- GlobalGiving charges $750-$900 to vet new (international) nonprofits; Pactman's Nonprofit Check Plus API costs $99-$166/mo. — search summaries of [GetChange help](https://intercom.help/getchange/en/articles/12675213-international-donations-globalgiving) and [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/nonprofit-check-plus-api-by-pactman/about)

### Inferences
- Peep has two architecture options: (1) intermediary/DAF model (like PayPal Giving Fund / Every.org) where a Peep-affiliated 501(c)(3) receives brand money, issues the receipt and re-grants — simplest for brands (one counterparty, one receipt) and for unverified small nonprofits; or (2) direct-to-nonprofit with Peep generating receipts on the nonprofit's behalf. Brand donors with set budgets will likely value option 1's consolidated receipting and compliance.
- Automated IRS BMF/Pub 78 checks plus Candid Seal status plus bank verification is the de facto stack; fiscally sponsored projects are a known edge case that broke GoFundMe's claim flow.

### Gaps
- Could not fetch GoFundMe's or Givebutter's own help docs on verification steps (blocked).
- No source found on how corporate donors (vs individuals) want receipts structured (e.g., sponsorship with benefits vs pure gift — quid pro quo rules); worth separate research.

## 5. What do users complain about, and what do small nonprofits wish existed?

### Takeaway
Top complaints: (1) "free" platforms' pre-checked donor tips (15-17%) that confuse donors and cause abandonment; (2) hidden or undisclosed fees (Givebutter class action, Aug 2026); (3) loss of control over their own brand/message (GoFundMe auto-pages); (4) grant databases that are expensive, capped, or hard to export (Candid); (5) payout holds. Small nonprofits want money they don't already have access to — "no big corporate sponsorship directory" exists — and simple tools without staff time.

### Cited Findings
- Zeffy: donors/nonprofits report confusion over a 15-17% pre-selected "optional contribution"; one nonprofit reported abandoned donations because of the 17% ask; a G2 reviewer called the default tip "sneaky"; an r/nonprofit user reported Zeffy held >$1,000 for nearly two months (aggregator and competitor sources). — [CheckThat Zeffy reviews](https://checkthat.ai/brands/zeffy/reviews); [Donorbox blog (competitor)](https://donorbox.org/nonprofit-blog/zeffy-alternatives); [4aGoodCause](https://4agoodcause.com/zeffy-vs-givebutter/)
- Givebutter: proposed class action filed Aug 4, 2026 in US District Court, S.D. California alleging it "dupes customers into paying for undisclosed processing fees on charitable contributions" and acts as a middleman "furtively charging excessive and undisclosed fees" (allegations, unproven). — [Bloomberg Law, Aug 5, 2026](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/software-platform-for-nonprofits-hit-with-deceptive-fees-suit)
- Givebutter user: cannot remove tips from events; "donors who didn't notice and thought that we had taken extra money." — [4aGoodCause](https://4agoodcause.com/zeffy-vs-givebutter/)
- Every.org: nonprofits cannot disable the tip prompt. — [Every.org blog](https://blog.every.org/how-does-every-org-work-your-top-10-pricing-questions-answered/)
- BetterWorld warns "optional-but-not-really" tip prompts add up; compare total donor cost, not headline fees. — [BetterWorld](https://betterworld.org/blog/fundraising-ideas/best-free-fundraising-platforms-for-nonprofits-in-2026/)
- GoFundMe auto-pages: nonprofits objected to lack of consent, inaccurate data, GoFundMe pages outranking nonprofits' own sites in Google, a pre-set 16.5% tip (later removed); "Consent and trust are really at the heart of the impact space." GoFundMe reversed to opt-in and de-indexed unclaimed pages (Oct 23, 2025). National Council of Nonprofits: tips and processing fees mean fewer resources than giving directly. — [Chronicle of Philanthropy](https://www.philanthropy.com/news/backlash-grows-as-charities-say-gofundmes-donation-pages-create-chaos/); [Yahoo News](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/gofundme-faces-backlash-creating-donation-180016881.html); [Minnesota Council of Nonprofits](https://minnesotanonprofits.org/?p=4641)
- Candid: Reddit users say "it's genuinely easier for me to look through pdf 990s manually than use Candid" and want Excel export of past grants rather than AI LOIs; exports historically limited to 100 rows at a time; bulk extraction prohibited by license. — [GrantCue citing Reddit and Candid](https://www.grantcue.com/blog/is-foundation-directory-being-discontinued)
- Kindful migration: custom reports must be rebuilt, registration forms don't transfer, crowdfunding module stops working. — [DonorDock (competitor)](https://www.donordock.com/articles/kindful-alternatives)
- Grant tools cost: most grant teams spend $200-$500/mo on disconnected tools (Grantable's claim); Grantable claims it gives nonprofits support that used to require a $3,000-$10,000 consultant. — [Grantable pricing](https://grantable.co/pricing); [Idealist listing](https://www.idealist.org/en/consultant/d4d4dbccc6394195904fa8cc3c7a0b1d-grantable-richmond)
- ZipSprout testimonial: "It's so hard to connect with companies and find out who is looking to do more philanthropy." — [ZipSprout](https://zipsprout.com/nonprofits-events-organization-sign-up/)
- M+R Benchmarks 2026: average online revenue up ~15% in 2025, concentrated in emergency-style giving (public media, food banks, rights orgs); one-time gifts grew faster than monthly; no small-org breakdown. — [M+R](https://www.mrss.com/lab/the-2026-mr-benchmarks-study-has-arrived/)

### Inferences
- Trust and fee transparency are the dominant emotional issues. Peep's promise of "posted free" will be scrutinized for hidden tips; a brand-funded or flat transparent fee on the funder side avoids the tip controversy entirely.
- Small nonprofits' stated pain is capacity (time, staff) and access (who funds?). Tools that require ongoing staff work (CRMs, grant pipelines) don't solve access; a "submit once, get matched" flow addresses both.
- Control over message is essential: nonprofits must approve the standardized one-pager before publishing.

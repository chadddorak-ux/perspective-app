# Sponsorship Marketplaces, Sponsorship-Sales Software and Sponsorship Intelligence Tools (as of 2026-10-23)

Method note: these notes come from about 40 web searches. Every WebFetch attempt failed with DNS errors (pulse2.com, globenewswire.com, financialcontent.com, utahbusiness.com, doublethedonation.com, app.sponsorpitch.com, varenyaz.com, sport-gsic.com), so the findings rest on search-result snippets and summaries, not full-page reads. The URLs point to the underlying pages. Many figures are vendor-reported or from aggregators, and they are flagged as such. "Dated" marks information that is probably stale.

Landscape map (my own segmentation, used throughout):
- **A. Sponsorship intelligence/data (mostly brand- and property-side research, sports and entertainment):** SponsorUnited, SponsorPitch, SponsorMind (part of it)
- **B. Property-side sales CRM/fulfillment ("partnership ops"):** SponsorCX, wehave, Sponsorworks, PandoPartner, Sponsy (creator/newsletter version)
- **C. Brand-side sponsorship portfolio management:** Trak, Sponsorium PerforMind
- **D. Two-sided marketplaces:** OpenSponsorship (athletes), SponsorMyEvent (events), Passionfroot (B2B creators), Collabstr/Aspire/GRIN (influencers, as an analog), SponsorGap (newsletters), ZipSprout (local nonprofits, brokered), and defunct or legacy ones (Sponsorist, Sponsorfied, Philanthropic.ly, Groupable)
- **E. Proposal/deck builders:** Sponseasy (dated), SponsorPitch proposals, SponsorMind's SponsorDeck, and generic tools (Qwilr, Proposify, Storydoc templates)
- **F. Measurement/valuation:** Relo Metrics, Zoomph, Hookit (now KORE)
- **G. Event platforms with sponsor modules:** Cvent, Eventbrite, Swapcard, Zeffy (nonprofit ticketing used to sell tiers), Double the Donation (nonprofit sponsorship pages)

## 1. Player profiles: what each does, who it serves, business model, scale, strengths, gaps

### Takeaway
Most well-funded players (SponsorUnited, SponsorCX, Relo, KORE/Hookit) serve professional sports and entertainment rights holders and big brands, and sell SaaS subscriptions. The two-sided marketplaces that exist serve athletes, creators and events and earn take rates of about 5-20%. I found no active, scaled marketplace that matches brands' giving budgets with nonprofit campaigns and also handles charitable receipts. Past attempts at a nonprofit sponsorship marketplace (Sponsorist, Philanthropic.ly, Groupable) look defunct.

### Cited Findings

**SponsorUnited (Stamford, CT; founded 2018): sponsorship intelligence for sports and entertainment**
- It is a B2B SaaS product for brands, rights holders (teams, leagues, venues, events), agencies and media companies to "discover, evaluate, negotiate, and activate" partnerships. Access is by paid subscription only — [SponsorUnited llms.txt](https://www.sponsorunited.com/llms.txt)

It includes a **Proposal Evaluator**: users "upload proposals for a detailed analysis" and get audience/market fit, competitive landscape, asset and pricing benchmarks, and a negotiation guide. 4.0 also added profiles enriched with audience demographics — [StreetInsider press release](https://www.streetinsider.com/Press+Releases/SponsorUnited+Redefines+Sponsorship+Decision+Making+with+Breakthrough+AI-Powered+Innovation/25952129.html); [SponsorUnited platform page](https://www.sponsorunited.com/platform)
- Vendor scale claim: brands can "benchmark pricing and validate spend with $45B+ in verified deal data." A 2024 report cites "over $13.3 billion in actual sponsorship deal transactions" in its pricing model, plus placement data from in-game and broadcast observation, team websites and announcements — [SponsorUnited platform](https://www.sponsorunited.com/platform); [SponsorUnited 2024 NA sports report](https://www.sponsorunited.com/reports/marketing-partnerships-across-north-american-sports-2024)
- Funding: $35M Series A led by Spectrum Equity (Nov 2022). Total-raised estimates range from $35M to $52.7M. Caplight's estimated valuation is about $100M (an estimate) — [The Daily Upside](https://www.thedailyupside.com/technology/sponsor-data-startup-raises-35-million-in-venture-capital/); [Owler](https://www.owler.com/company/sponsorunited/funding); [Caplight](https://www.caplight.com/company/sponsorunited)
- June 17, 2026: appointed Tim Braz as its first Chief Commercial Officer, framed "As AI Transforms Sponsorship" — [GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/06/17/3313473/0/en/As-AI-Transforms-Sponsorship-SponsorUnited-Appoints-Tim-Braz-as-First-Chief-Commercial-Officer.html)

- It publishes content aimed at nonprofits ("nonprofits can build mission-driven sponsorships"), but its product focus is sports and entertainment — [SponsorUnited insights](https://www.sponsorunited.com/insights/nonprofits-can-build-mission-driven-sponsorships)

### Inferences
- The most money and data sit in pro sports (SponsorUnited's $45B deal dataset, SponsorCX's USL and Clemson wins, Relo and KORE's computer vision). The small nonprofit segment (a $250-$5,000 sponsorship, typically sold through a Zeffy ticket page or a PDF) is served only by generic fundraising tools and brokers like ZipSprout.
- The repeated failure or disappearance of nonprofit sponsorship marketplaces (Sponsorist, Philanthropic.ly, Groupable, Sponsorfied) suggests cold-start and liquidity problems. Brand demand did not reliably show up for long-tail listings. Peep should plan the demand side explicitly, for example with budget-first brand onboarding and SMB local-giving use cases.
- Creator marketplaces (Collabstr, Passionfroot) are the closest working model for Peep's transaction layer: standardized listings, upfront prices, escrow, brokered payment, tax and invoicing handled, and take rates of 5-15%.

### Gaps
- No information found on "Spotlight" as a nonprofit sponsorship marketplace, on "Sponsor.com" as a product, or on "Sponsorbase." These may not exist as named or may be very small.
- Sponseasy's and SponsorMyEvent's current status (2025-2026) is unknown.
- No pricing for SponsorUnited, Zoomph, Relo, SponsorCX, Sponsorium or SponsorMind.
- Givebutter's sponsorship features were not surfaced by search.

## 2. How do tools help properties create sponsorship offers (templates, deck builders, AI)? Does any convert existing PDFs/decks?

### Takeaway
Offer creation is mostly form-based package builders (SponsorMyEvent, Cvent, Swapcard, Zeffy ticket tiers) or deck builders (Sponseasy, SponsorPitch proposals, SponsorMind's SponsorDeck). The only "upload your PDF proposal" feature I found is SponsorUnited's Proposal Evaluator, and it runs on the brand/evaluation side: it scores and benchmarks a proposal rather than turning it into a polished, standardized listing. I found no tool that converts a nonprofit's existing PDF or deck into a standardized campaign page.

### Cited Findings
- SponsorUnited 4.0 Proposal Evaluator: "Upload proposals for a detailed analysis" covering audience and market fit, competitive landscape, asset and pricing benchmarks, and negotiation guidance — [SponsorUnited platform](https://www.sponsorunited.com/platform); [StreetInsider](https://www.streetinsider.com/Press+Releases/SponsorUnited+Redefines+Sponsorship+Decision+Making+with+Breakthrough+AI-Powered+Innovation/25952129.html)
- Sponseasy: deck "in minutes," with auto-import from Eventbrite event data, link or embed sharing, and engagement analytics (dated, about 2014) — [BizBash](https://www.bizbash.com/production-strategy/strategy/media-gallery/13482076/4-systems-to-streamline-your-event-sponsorship-strategy)
- SponsorPitch "SP Proposals" added video, demographics and pricing tools (details unverified) — [SponsorPitch](https://app.sponsorpitch.com/posts/new-on-sponsorpitch-proposals-video-demographics-and-pricing-tools)
- SponsorMind's SponsorDeck (proposals) plus SponsorValue (valuation) target small and mid-sized event organizers (May 2026) — [GSIC](https://www.sport-gsic.com/blog/new-members-3/sponsormind-joins-gsic-bringing-industrytrained-ai-to-transform-the-global-sponsorship-ecosystem-137)
- PandoPartner uses AI to "accelerate research, packaging, sales materials" — [rfp.wiki](https://www.rfp.wiki/marketing/sponsorship-management-platforms/sponsorcx/alternatives-and-competitors)
- SponsorMyEvent: organizers fill in a profile with sponsorship packages and prices, which staff approve before it goes live — [Skift](https://meetings.skift.com/reviews/sponsormyevent-matchmaking-marketplace-events-sponsors-review/)
- Cvent: package bundles of features and consumables. Swapcard: "tailored sponsorship tiers" and premium add-ons — [Cvent](https://release.cvent.com/eventmanagement/announcements/spotlight-exhibitor-sponsor-packages); [Swapcard](https://www.swapcard.com/solutions/event-revenue-growth)
- Generic proposal templates are widely used: Qwilr, Proposify, Dock, Storydoc, Craft, Taskade, and a free AI "sponsorship proposal generator" (InsertChat). beehiiv has built-in direct sponsorship proposals for newsletters — [Qwilr](https://qwilr.com/create/sponsorship-proposal); [Proposify](https://proposify.com/proposal-templates/sponsorship-proposal-template); [Storydoc](https://storydoc.com/templates/sponsorship-tier-deck); [InsertChat](https://insertchat.com/free-tools/sponsorship-proposal-generator); [beehiiv](https://www.beehiiv.com/support/article/31649457623319)
- Typical small-nonprofit tiers on Zeffy are free-text ticket descriptions (logo placement, comp tickets, signage) priced at $250-$1,500 — [Zeffy](https://www.zeffy.com/en-US/ticketing/14th-annual-mv-beer-festival-sponsorship)
- Practitioner advice: long emails with big attached decks usually get ignored. A short note with three fit points works better — [Nunify](https://www.nunify.com/blogs/how-to-find-sponsors-for-an-event)

### Inferences
- "PDF/deck in, standardized one-pager out" looks like open whitespace. The closest analogs either evaluate PDFs for buyers (SponsorUnited) or build decks from structured event data (Sponseasy from Eventbrite). Peep's ingestion feature would be differentiated, especially if the output is normalized (consistent tier schema, cause tags, audience fields) so it can feed matching.
- Practitioner advice against big attached decks supports a standardized, skimmable one-pager format for brands.

### Gaps
- Could not verify whether SponsorMind's SponsorDeck or SponsorPitch's proposal tool accepts uploaded PDFs.
- No data on how many nonprofits already have a sponsorship deck or PDF.

## 3. How do brands discover opportunities? Is there matching on goals, audience or budget? Is it two-sided?

### Takeaway
Discovery splits into (a) intelligence databases where brands and properties research each other but transact off-platform (SponsorUnited, SponsorPitch), (b) filterable marketplaces with basic matching by location, type, budget or sport (SponsorMyEvent, OpenSponsorship, Collabstr), and (c) brokered matching (ZipSprout, SponsorPitch SponsorEngage, OpenSponsorship managed plans). "AI matching" claims are new (SponsorMind's SponsorHitch, DoSponsor, the CauseConnect prototype). I found no budget-first "set a giving budget, pick causes, get matched" flow for brands funding nonprofits.

### Cited Findings
- SponsorUnited targets brands that want to "discover white space and evaluate properties" and "benchmark pricing" — [SponsorUnited platform](https://www.sponsorunited.com/platform)
- SponsorPitch Professional includes "sponsor recommendations." Brands use it to research competitors and find opportunities, and sellers to reach brands — [SponsorPitch pricing](https://app.sponsorpitch.com/pricing); [Why SponsorPitch](https://sponsorpitch.com/why-sponsorpitch)
- SponsorMyEvent: sponsors browse by location, event type and budget, and organizers can also invite their existing sponsors — [Skift](https://meetings.skift.com/reviews/sponsormyevent-matchmaking-marketplace-events-sponsors-review/)
- OpenSponsorship: brands post campaigns and search a database of 20k+ athletes across 160 sports — [Subscribed.fyi](https://subscribed.fyi/opensponsorship/reviews/)
- SponsorMind SponsorHitch matches using event data, audience profiles, asset performance and brand objectives — [GSIC](https://www.sport-gsic.com/blog/new-members-3/sponsormind-joins-gsic-bringing-industrytrained-ai-to-transform-the-global-sponsorship-ecosystem-137)
- Passionfroot: creator storefronts (inbound) plus a brokered Partner Network (15% sourcing fee) — [Passionfroot help](https://help.passionfroot.me/creators/getting-started/pricing)
- ZipSprout builds campaigns "based on your brand's marketing goals" and charges per opportunity found — [Citation Labs](https://citationlabs.com/?p=1957)
- Philanthropic.ly (status unknown) matched nonprofit sponsorships to companies "based on their giving initiatives," which is the closest historical precedent to Peep's matching — [3BL Media](https://www.3blmedia.com/news/introducing-philanthropicly-new-giving-platform-creating-meaningful-connections-between)

- On the property side, the main discovery method is still outbound: LinkedIn searches for "Director of Partnerships," checking who sponsored similar events, and warm intros. Cold outreach has low hit rates, especially for new events — [Cvent blog, June 2026](https://www.cvent.com/en/blog/events/how-to-get-event-sponsors); [Nunify](https://www.nunify.com/blogs/how-to-find-sponsors-for-an-event); [EventBuzz podcast](https://www.buzzsprout.com/1389142/episodes/9206427-35-event-partnerships-sponsors-speakers-contracts)

### Inferences
- Existing marketplaces match on marketing variables (audience, reach, location, sport). None found match on cause or mission, giving budget, or impact. That gives Peep a distinct axis: budget plus cause plus geography plus verification.
- Intelligence platforms are effectively one-sided research tools. True two-sided liquidity exists mainly in creator marketplaces.

### Gaps
- No evidence on match quality or conversion rates for any marketplace.
- Couldn't verify whether SponsorMyEvent still has active brand demand.

## 4. Pricing models (subscriptions, take rates, listing fees)

### Takeaway
There are three models. Enterprise SaaS (quote-based, about $5k-$40k+/yr: SponsorUnited, SponsorCX, Trak, Aspire, measurement vendors). Low-cost self-serve SaaS ($29-$350/mo: SponsorPitch, Sponsy, Sponseasy, Collabstr Premium, GRIN). Marketplace take rates of 5-20%, often charged to whichever side has the money (Passionfroot 5%/15% to brands, Collabstr 10% to brands, OpenSponsorship 10-20% from athletes, SponsorMyEvent 10-15%, Sponsorfied 10-25%). Listing is generally free for the supply side.

### Cited Findings

- Enterprise pricing at $5k+/yr leaves sub-$10k-budget nonprofits and SMB sponsors unserved.

### Gaps
- Current, verified rates for OpenSponsorship and SponsorMyEvent. GRIN's 2026 repricing is from a single secondary source.

## 5. After the deal: contracts, payments, fulfillment proof, asset collection, reporting

### Takeaway
Post-deal tooling is strongest in two places: property-side ops software (SponsorCX, wehave, Sponsorworks, PandoPartner) and brand-side portfolio tools (Trak), both built around contract obligations, fulfillment tracking and proof-of-performance libraries, increasingly with AI. Marketplaces handle payment through escrow released on deliverable approval (OpenSponsorship, Collabstr, Sponsorist's 7-day hold). Paid measurement (Relo, Zoomph, Hookit) values logo exposure for large properties. I found no tool for small nonprofits that bundles transfer, verification, logo and brand-asset collection, proof and a receipt.

### Cited Findings
- OpenSponsorship holds brand payments in escrow "until both parties are happy." Release requires brand approval of all deliverables, with a full refund if the deal doesn't execute. Disputes go to email support — [OpenSponsorship help](https://help.opensponsorship.com/en/articles/763001-how-do-you-guarantee-that-the-athlete-other-talent-sticks-to-the-terms-in-my-campaign); [OpenSponsorship help 2](https://help.opensponsorship.com/en/articles/763052-what-happens-if-an-athlete-bails-after-payment-was-sent)
- Collabstr: escrow until content is delivered, local-currency payouts, "tax and invoicing handled" — [IT Brief](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/collabstr-adds-snapchat-creators-to-brand-marketplace)
- SponsorMyEvent: in-app negotiation, contract sharing and payments — [Skift](https://meetings.skift.com/reviews/sponsormyevent-matchmaking-marketplace-events-sponsors-review/)
- Sponsorist (dated): held funds 7 days "while the sponsored and sponsor connected" to exchange materials, then paid out and sent the charity share as a donation — [THAT Agency](https://thatagency.com/blog/behind-the-scenes-at-sponsorist-com-interview-with-rosston-meyer/)
- SponsorMind: SponsorDeal (agreements) and SponsorPay (payments and invoicing) — [GSIC](https://www.sport-gsic.com/blog/new-members-3/sponsormind-joins-gsic-bringing-industrytrained-ai-to-transform-the-global-sponsorship-ecosystem-137)
- SponsorCX: fulfillment evidence and proof-of-performance records support "reporting and renewal conversations" — [rfp.wiki](https://www.rfp.wiki/vendors/sponsorcx)
- wehave: "contract to proof of delivery," plus data to show sponsors the value of their investment — [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/wehave)
- Sponsorworks: AI extraction of obligations from contracts and proof-of-performance reporting — [rfp.wiki](https://www.rfp.wiki/vendors/sponsorworks-partnerships-platform)
- Trak AI Proof Intelligence: logo detection, transcription and summaries of proof uploads, rolled up by community focus or activation type — [Trak AI](https://www.trak.io/trak-ai-for-sponsorship-activation)
- Relo Metrics, Zoomph and Hookit convert logo and brand exposure (broadcast, streaming, social) into dollar value with computer vision — [Relo](https://press.relometrics.com/relo-metrics-accelerates-ai-powered-sponsorship-analytics-for-sports-entertainment-with-computer-vision-and-multi-modal-ai-powered-by-nvidia); [Zoomph](https://zoomph.com/?p=56493); [SportBusiness Hookit](https://www.sportbusiness.com/organisation/hookit/)

- Swapcard and Cvent give sponsors lead retrieval and booth analytics as ROI evidence — [Swapcard](https://www.swapcard.com/event-lead-generation); [Cvent](https://hello.cvent.com/en-drive-sponsorship-webinar-recording.html)

### Inferences
- Asset collection (logos, brand guidelines) appears only implicitly: Sponsorist's material-exchange window and the "customer portals" in Sponsy and SponsorCX. A built-in "sponsor uploads logo and guidelines, nonprofit confirms placement with photo proof" loop sized for small orgs would be new.
- Escrow released on proof is an established pattern that Peep could adapt, e.g. release on verification or on proof of recognition. For pure charitable gifts, though, holding funds may create legal and fiduciary questions (see section 6).

### Gaps
- No detail found on how marketplaces collect brand assets or on standardized proof formats for small properties.

## 6. Do any handle tax-deductible charitable sponsorships vs marketing sponsorships, receipts, or nonprofit verification?

### Takeaway
I found no sponsorship marketplace or sales tool that explicitly distinguishes IRS "qualified sponsorship payments" (acknowledgment only, treated as charitable or non-UBIT) from advertising (taxable to the nonprofit as UBIT), generates donor or sponsor tax receipts, or verifies 501(c)(3) status. Sports and creator marketplaces treat every deal as a marketing purchase. This is a clear gap and a compliance minefield that Peep would need to design for.

### Cited Findings
- Under IRC §513(i), a "qualified sponsorship payment" is one where the sponsor receives no substantial return benefit beyond use or acknowledgment of its name, logo or product lines. Advertising, meaning "messages containing qualitative or comparative language, price information, endorsements or inducements to buy," falls outside the safe harbor. Payments can be split into qualifying and non-qualifying portions — [IRS: Advertising or qualified sponsorship payments?](https://www.irs.gov/node/13726); [Federal Register final regs 2002](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2002-04-25/pdf/02-9930.pdf)
- Practitioner guidance: calls to action or promotional messaging in an agreement can turn that portion into advertising income that may trigger UBIT. A sponsor "can be acknowledged… but not promoted" — [PYA](https://www.pyapc.com/insights/the-thin-green-line-sponsorships-versus-advertising/); [AAFRC](https://aafrc.org/is-a-corporate-sponsorship-a-donation-or-advertising-revenue/); [Venable](https://www.venable.com/Corporate-Sponsorship--The-Final-Regulations-05-01-2002)
- Typical small-nonprofit tiers sold on Zeffy mix acknowledgment (logo on website and signage) with tangible benefits (comp tickets, promo items), which affects deductibility — [Zeffy](https://www.zeffy.com/en-US/ticketing/14th-annual-mv-beer-festival-sponsorship)
- Collabstr "handles tax and invoicing," but that covers creator income and invoicing, not charitable receipts — [IT Brief](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/collabstr-adds-snapchat-creators-to-brand-marketplace)
- Sponsorist routed a percentage of each sale to a charity from its nonprofit directory "as a donation," an early hybrid of marketing and charity (dated) — [THAT Agency](https://thatagency.com/blog/behind-the-scenes-at-sponsorist-com-interview-with-rosston-meyer/)
- Trak and Sponsorium PerforMind manage "community investment" alongside sponsorship on the corporate side, so brands do track cause-related spend separately from marketing sponsorship in these tools — [Trak AI](https://www.trak.io/trak-ai-for-sponsorship-activation); [SI Lab](https://www.thesilab.com/vendor-directory/sponsorium-sponsorship-management)

### Inferences
- Peep's "no communication required, transfer + verification + tax receipt" model maps naturally onto the qualified-sponsorship-payment safe harbor (acknowledgment only). Peep's tier builder could flag benefits that tip a payment into advertising (comparative claims, CTAs, comp tickets with fair market value) and split receipts accordingly. No competitor found does this.
- Whether a brand books the payment as a charitable contribution (corporate deduction limits) or as a business expense (marketing) is a brand-side accounting choice. Supporting both labels on receipts could be a selling point to brand finance teams.
- Nonprofit verification (IRS status, standing) is standard in CSR and giving platforms (out of my scope, likely covered by another researcher) but absent from the sponsorship tools reviewed.

### Gaps
- Did not research CSR and workplace-giving platforms (Benevity, Bonterra/CyberGrants, Goodera) or DAF-style intermediaries, which do handle verification and receipts. They are likely adjacent competitors for Peep's brand side.
- No source found on how any sponsorship platform treats quid-pro-quo disclosure (benefits over $75) for sponsorship tiers.

## 8. Cross-cutting: what is missing for small nonprofits and for brands

### Takeaway
For small nonprofits: there is no affordable tool that turns existing materials into a credible, standardized offer, puts it in front of brands with budget, and handles payment, verification, receipts and proof. Today they use Zeffy or Givebutter ticket pages, PDFs and cold email. For brands, especially SMB and mid-market: there is no budget-first, cause-first discovery of vetted nonprofit opportunities with standardized pricing, compliance-aware receipts and low-touch fulfillment proof. Existing brand tools are either $5k+/yr portfolio managers for sponsorships they already have (Trak, PerforMind) or sports and entertainment intelligence (SponsorUnited).

### Cited Findings
- Small-nonprofit sponsorships run $250-$1,500 per tier and are sold as ticket types with free-text benefits — [Zeffy examples](https://www.zeffy.com/en-US/ticketing/sfw-4th-annual-golf-outing-sponsorships-and-donations)
- The only scaled local-nonprofit matcher found (ZipSprout) is driven by brand SEO and link goals, with about $1M placed across 1,300+ opportunities (self-reported) — [Citation Labs](https://citationlabs.com/?p=1957)
- Enterprise ops and portfolio tools cost $5k-$25k+/yr or are quote-only — [Trak](https://www.trak.io/pricing); [rfp.wiki](https://www.rfp.wiki/vendors/sponsorcx)
- Nonprofit guides tell orgs to show how a partnership "adds value to the company's bottom line" and to research CSR fit manually — [Double the Donation](https://doublethedonation.com/corporate-sponsorships-guide/); [Grapevine](https://www.grapevine.org/blog/how-to-secure-corporate-sponsorships)
- Brands' corporate tools track "community investment" alongside sponsorships, so a cause-sponsorship budget line exists inside companies — [Trak AI](https://www.trak.io/trak-ai-for-sponsorship-activation); [SI Lab Sponsorium](https://www.thesilab.com/vendor-directory/sponsorium-sponsorship-management)
- Precedents for nonprofit sponsorship marketplaces (Sponsorist, Philanthropic.ly, Groupable) appear inactive — [THAT Agency](https://thatagency.com/blog/behind-the-scenes-at-sponsorist-com-interview-with-rosston-meyer/); [3BL Media](https://www.3blmedia.com/news/introducing-philanthropicly-new-giving-platform-creating-meaningful-connections-between); [NonProfit Pro](https://stagingcbi.nonprofitpro.com/?p=4057)

### Inferences
- Peep's main differentiators against this set:
  1. PDF/deck ingestion into a standardized one-pager (no competitor found).
  2. Matching on cause, budget, geography and verification rather than audience reach.
  3. A transaction layer that handles 513(i)-aware receipts and nonprofit verification.
  4. Optional communication, so it is low-touch for brands.
- The closest competitive threats are probably adjacent, not direct:
  1. Nonprofit payment platforms (Zeffy, Givebutter, Double the Donation's sponsorship pages) adding brand discovery.
  2. SponsorMind-style AI suites moving down-market into nonprofits.
  3. CSR and giving platforms (Benevity and similar) adding sponsorship-style recognition.
  4. Creator marketplaces (Passionfroot, Collabstr), whose storefront, escrow and take-rate playbook could be pointed at causes.
- Biggest risk shown by the history: demand-side liquidity. Brands with small budgets have not historically self-served on nonprofit sponsorship marketplaces, so Peep likely needs curated supply and budget-based brand acquisition, e.g. SMBs that want local visibility or brands with year-end giving budgets.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data found on total US small-nonprofit sponsorship spend or on SMB brand willingness to self-serve.
- CSR, workplace-giving and DAF platforms were not researched here and should be covered elsewhere for the brand-side comparison.
